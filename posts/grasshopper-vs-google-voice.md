It is 2:15 on a Thursday afternoon. A landscaper is ankle-deep in mulch with a blower in one hand when his phone buzzes against his thigh. The screen says "Unknown." It is a homeowner who wants a quote on a retaining wall, and the number on his yard sign is the same number his kid's school uses to call him.

He lets it ring out. The homeowner does not leave a voicemail. By the time he looks at the phone, she has called the next name on the list.

That job is why people go shopping for a second phone line. Grasshopper and Google Voice are the two names that come up first, and they are priced low enough that most owners never compare them properly.

## The short answer

**Pick Google Voice** if you want the cheapest separate business number, you already live in Google's apps, and you are fine answering calls yourself or letting them go to voicemail. Google's free Basic plan costs $0 and paid plans start at $10 a month (Google Workspace pricing page, 12 October 2026).

**Pick Grasshopper** if you want a number built for small businesses with extensions and a free trial before you commit. Its pricing page showed plans starting at $14 a month on 12 October 2026. Grasshopper's other plan prices were not visible when we looked, so check the tier you need.

**Skip both** if the problem is the call you cannot pick up. Both give you a business number that rings your phone. Neither pricing page we reviewed lists anything that answers the call for you at 2:15 with a blower running.

:::stats
$0 :: Google Voice Basic, individuals only, existing mobile number required (Google, 12 October 2026)
$10/mo :: Google Voice Starter, the first paid tier (Google, 12 October 2026)
$14/mo :: Grasshopper's starting price (Grasshopper pricing page, 12 October 2026)
7 days :: Grasshopper free trial, no credit card required (Grasshopper, 12 October 2026)
:::

## What each one is

### Grasshopper

Grasshopper sells a business phone number that works with the phone you already own. Its pricing page says it has served over 400,000 customers, which is the company's own figure. The customer quotes on that page describe taking calls from wherever the owner is, managing voicemail, and sending text messages from the business number.

The page also lists questions it answers in an FAQ: adding extensions or numbers on top of a plan, whether there is a contract, whether international calls are included in the unlimited minutes, professional voice recordings for greetings, and nonprofit discounts. The answers did not show when we fetched the page. Read them before you pay, particularly the contract question.

### Google Voice

Google Voice is part of Google Workspace's product line and comes in two families.

The individual plans:

- **Basic** costs $0. You get one number for all your devices and unlimited domestic calling and texting, subject to Google's acceptable use policy. It requires an existing mobile number.
- **Starter** costs $10 a month. It adds 24/7 access to Google Voice support, three-way calling, call transfers and call recording. Your number will not be reclaimed for inactivity.
- **Standard** lists at $20 a month, with a 50% discount for the first six months, so $10 during that period. It adds auto attendants and Gemini notes on calls.

The team plans are priced per user and require a separate Google Workspace plan:

- **Starter** costs $10 per user per month, for up to 10 users.
- **Standard** costs $20 per user per month and adds ring groups and call queuing.
- **Premier** costs $30 per user per month and adds automatic call recording, transcription and AI notes.

Google's page also says Voice blocks spam calls and transcribes voicemail to text.

## Grasshopper vs Google Voice, side by side

Everything below comes from each company's own pricing page as fetched on 12 October 2026. Prices move, so treat these as a dated snapshot.

**Entry price**
- Grasshopper starts at $14 a month.
- Google Voice starts at $0 for Basic, or $10 a month for the first paid tier.

**Free trial**
- Grasshopper offers 7 days with no credit card.
- Google Voice Basic is free, which works as a permanent trial of the basics.

**Who it is built for**
- Grasshopper is aimed at small businesses.
- Google Voice covers individuals and teams, and the team plans assume you already pay for Google Workspace.

**Getting more people on the line**
- Grasshopper's FAQ mentions adding extensions on top of a plan. The answer was not visible.
- Google Voice team plans are priced per user, with ring groups on Standard.

**Menus and routing**
- Google Voice Standard includes auto attendants.
- Grasshopper's routing features were not detailed on the page we fetched.

**Support and call recording**
- Google Voice Starter adds 24/7 support and call recording.
- Grasshopper's page did not list recording or support hours when we fetched it.

**What happens to a call you miss**
- Grasshopper's customer quotes mention voicemail and storing messages.
- Google Voice transcribes voicemail to text.
- Neither pricing page lists a service that picks up and talks to the caller.

If you are buying on price alone, Google Voice Basic wins, and it is hard to argue with free. If you are buying on small-business polish and a trial before you pay, Grasshopper is the more obvious fit. Neither answer touches the missed call.

## How to choose between them

Four questions settle most of it.

:::steps
Is it just you or a team? :: Solo owners can run on Google Voice Basic or Starter. Once two or three people need to share a number, Google's per-user pricing and the separate Workspace subscription start to add up, so price Grasshopper's extension option against it.
Do you already use Google Workspace? :: If yes, Google Voice slots into what you pay for. If no, remember the team plans require Workspace, billed separately.
Do you need recording or a menu? :: Google Voice Starter adds call recording and Standard adds auto attendants. Check what Grasshopper includes at the tier you would buy.
Can you stand the voicemail? :: If a missed call costs you a few dollars of mild annoyance, voicemail is fine. If it costs you a retaining wall, the line itself is not the fix.
:::

Run the 2:15 test before you pay for either. Sign up for the free trial or the free plan, put the number on your phone, and then call it from a second phone while you are doing something that keeps your hands busy. If you would not pick up in that moment, you have learned what the line will do on a real day.

## Where both fall short for a one-truck business

Both products solve a privacy problem. They give you a number to put on the truck so you are not handing out your personal cell. That has real value, and it is why people buy them.

They do not solve the availability problem. A new number that rings your phone rings in your pocket exactly as often as the old one. The homeowner still hits voicemail while you are under a deck.

Owners usually respond in one of three ways.

**They add an answering service.** A live service takes a message and passes it on. We compared several in [the best answering services for small business](/blog/best-answering-service-for-small-business). Prices run into the hundreds of dollars a month for live human coverage. Ruby's pricing page, for example, listed $250 a month for 50 receptionist minutes and $395 for 100 on 12 October 2026. Smith.ai's page listed a $300 a month starter plan for 30 calls on the same date.

**They set up a missed-call text.** Sending the caller a text within seconds gives them a reason to wait. We cover the mechanics in [our missed-call text-back guide](/blog/missed-call-text-back). Texting from a business number runs into carrier registration rules.

**They try an AI receptionist.** This is the newer option, and we lay out how it works in [the AI receptionist guide for small businesses](/blog/ai-receptionist-for-small-business). It picks up and talks to the caller, and it hands the details to you.

## So which one should you buy

- **You want a free second number and nothing else.** Google Voice Basic.
- **You are a solo owner who answers your own phone and wants a separate number.** Google Voice Starter at $10 a month, or Grasshopper from $14 a month if the free trial and small-business setup appeal to you.
- **You run a team and already pay for Google Workspace.** Google Voice team plans, priced per user.

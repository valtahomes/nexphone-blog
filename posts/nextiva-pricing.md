It is 6:12 on a Thursday morning. The owner of a two-van HVAC shop is at the kitchen table with last month’s phone bill, a Nextiva tab open, and a coffee that has gone cold. The page says $15 a user. The quote in his inbox has more lines than that. He has twenty minutes before the first job, and he needs the number he will actually pay.

:::callout
**The $15 figure is the phone system.**
XBert, extra users, and month-to-month billing sit on other lines. Add those before you compare Nextiva to an answering service, or you are comparing two different products.
:::

## The short answer

**Nextiva is a per-user business phone system**, not a per-call answering service. As of **5 October 2026** on [Nextiva’s pricing page](https://www.nextiva.com/nextiva-pricing), the small-business plans are:

- **Core** — **$15 per user per month** billed annually, **$23** billed month-to-month (the page shows 35% off)
- **Engage** — **$25 per user per month** billed annually, **$50** billed month-to-month (50% off)
- **Scale** — **$75 per user per month**, demo required; no monthly/annual split is shown

**XBert**, Nextiva’s AI receptionist, is an add-on. The same page advertises **XBert AI plans from $99 a month**. A one-person shop that only needs a number will see a small bill. A shop that needs the phone answered while both hands are in a unit is buying the phone system plus the AI. That is a different number.

Contact-center plans start at **$75 per agent per month** for Essential. Those are a different product line. Ignore them unless you are staffing a queue.

Prices move. Treat this as a snapshot of that page on that date, and ask sales to put every add-on in writing before you sign.

## What Nextiva is selling

Nextiva sells a cloud phone system: inbound and outbound voice, business SMS, a mobile app, call routing, video meetings and team chat. That is the Core list on 5 October 2026. Engage adds customer-to-team SMS, a toll-free number and minutes, advanced reporting, live chat and a chatbot. Scale is pitched as a customer-experience platform: blended inbound and outbound, AI transcription and summary, skills-based routing, journey orchestration.

XBert is the piece most home-service owners came for. Nextiva’s XBert product page (fetched the same day) says it greets callers, books appointments, captures leads, routes to a person when it should, and handles texts and web chat. It is listed as an add-on on Core, Engage and Scale, not as something sitting inside the $15.

People search **nextiva pricing** because they saw a low per-user number. People who cannot pick up the phone at 2pm on a roof need the add-on. Price both, or the comparison against an answering service is nonsense.

If you already know you want the line answered and you are picking a vendor, [the best answering services for small business](/blog/best-answering-service-for-small-business) is the buying guide.

## The three small-business plans

All three include a business phone and team messaging. AI is something you add. Figures below are from Nextiva’s pricing page on **5 October 2026**.

**Core — $15/user/mo annual, $23/user/mo monthly**

Nextiva’s label is “connect your team and customers.” The public feature list:

- Inbound and outbound voice
- Business SMS
- Video meetings
- Screen and file sharing
- Call routing
- Team chat
- Mobile app
- XBert AI receptionist as an add-on

This is the one-person or tiny-office plan. One seat at $15 a month on annual billing is $180 a year for the phone system. Same seat month-to-month is $276 a year. The gap is the annual toggle, not a different product.

**Engage — $25/user/mo annual, $50/user/mo monthly**

Nextiva marks this most popular. Everything in Core, plus:

- Customer-to-team SMS
- Toll-free number and minutes
- Advanced reporting
- Live chat and chatbot
- XBert as an add-on

The monthly price is double the annual price. Three people on Engage, paid monthly, is **$150 a month** before XBert. Same three on annual billing is **$75 a month**. If a [toll-free number](/blog/toll-free-number-for-business) is why you opened this tab, Engage is the first small-business plan that lists one.

**Scale — $75/user/mo, demo required**

Nextiva’s copy is “get everything in one package.” The public list on top of Engage-class features:

- Voice and web chat
- Blended inbound and outbound
- AI transcription and summary
- Skills-based routing
- Journey orchestration
- XBert as an add-on

There is no month-to-month figure on the page. “Get a demo” usually means the number you saw is a starting point, not a cart total. Treat $75 as the published floor per user, then get the quote.

A promo banner on the same date offered “up to 12 months free” to switch, with an asterisk. Promos are not list prices. Read the term, the seat minimum, and what happens in month 13 before you use that number in a spreadsheet.

Nextiva also prints a 30-day money-back guarantee, cancel anytime, and no credit card required for a demo. Those are their terms on that page, not a review of how refunds actually run.

## XBert, the line that changes the bill

Without XBert, Nextiva is a phone system you or your crew still have to answer. With XBert, it is a phone system plus an AI receptionist.

What Nextiva says XBert does, from the XBert product page on 5 October 2026:

- Answers inbound calls 24/7
- Books appointments against a calendar
- Captures lead details and can follow up by text
- Transfers to a person with context when the caller needs a human
- Works across calls, texts and web chat
- Sends transcripts and summaries after the call

What the pricing page says it costs: **AI plans from $99 a month**, listed next to “unlimited calling and XBert AI plans from $99/mo.” There is no public tier sheet with included minutes, overage, or a per-call rate for XBert on that page.

For a small shop:

- Phone system: Core or Engage, times the number of people who need a seat
- Answering: XBert from $99 a month, pending a written quote
- Total for a solo owner who wants the AI: roughly **$114 a month** on annual Core ($15 + $99) or **$122** on month-to-month Core ($23 + $99), before taxes, extra numbers, or anything sales adds later

That $99 is a “from.” If your volume is high, expect the quote to move. Get the XBert line item in writing. Then compare it to other [AI receptionist pricing](/blog/ai-receptionist-pricing), which is billed in a different shape, often a flat monthly with a minutes cap, or a per-call rate.

## Contact-center plans, so you do not mix them up

Further down the same page, Nextiva lists contact-center tiers:

- **Essential** — from **$75 per agent per month**
- **Professional** — contact sales
- **Premium** — contact sales

Essential’s public list includes omnichannel, journey orchestration, AI transcription and summary, skills-based routing, voice and web chat. Professional adds self-service, supervisor tools, PCI-DSS payments, and progressive/predictive dialing. Premium adds workforce management, unlimited channels, and in-conversation payments.

A two-van trade shop is not this buyer. If a sales call starts quoting per-agent contact-center numbers, you have left the small-business plans. Come back to Core or Engage, or leave the call.

## What the public page does not price

Nextiva’s small-business grid is per user, per month. It does not, on 5 October 2026, publish a list for:

- Extra local numbers
- Number porting
- Hardware (desk phones, headsets)
- Carrier registration for outbound business texting
- XBert minutes, overage, or per-call rates
- Taxes and recovery fees

US outbound business SMS usually needs A2P 10DLC registration. Nextiva’s pricing page does not itemise that. Do not assume it is free, and do not assume it is included in Core’s “business SMS” bullet. Ask.

Porting is the usual path if you want to keep the number on the van and the Google listing. The page talks about switching with temporary numbers and “no downtime” in customer quotes. It does not list a port fee or a typical timeline. Ask for both in the quote, in days, not in adjectives.

If the quote is still a per-user phone system and you wanted a receptionist, you are in the wrong aisle. That is a category miss, not a coupon problem.

## What a small shop actually pays

Two worked examples. Seats at the public rates above. XBert at the advertised **from $99 a month**. No tax, no extra numbers, no hardware.

**One person, one van**

You need a business number so your personal cell stays private, and you need the line answered while you are under a sink.

- Core, annual, 1 seat: **$15/mo**
- Core, monthly, 1 seat: **$23/mo**
- Core annual + XBert from $99: **from $114/mo**
- Core monthly + XBert from $99: **from $122/mo**

The phone system alone is cheap. The phone system that answers itself is a different product, and most of that bill is XBert.

**Three people, two vans, one office phone**

Three seats, because three people need the app.

- Engage, annual, 3 seats: **$75/mo**
- Engage, monthly, 3 seats: **$150/mo**
- Either of those + XBert from $99: **from $174/mo** annual, **from $249/mo** monthly

Pay attention to the toggle. Engage’s monthly rate is $50 a head. Three heads is $150 before the AI. Annual halves it. If you cannot commit to a year, the screenshot price is not your price.

Missing one after-hours emergency still dwarfs either invoice for most trades. That is the comparison that belongs on the kitchen table, next to the quote: coverage versus one lost job, not Core versus Engage.

## Other ways to buy an answered phone

Nextiva is one billing shape. The others you will see while you shop, with prices fetched **5 October 2026**:

**Per-user cloud phone (Nextiva and the same aisle)**

You pay for seats every month, quiet or busy. Cheap for a number and an app. The bill grows when you add people and when you add the AI pack. [RingCentral’s pricing](/blog/ringcentral-pricing) lives in this same per-user shape if you are comparing phone systems to phone systems.

**Per-minute live receptionist**

Ruby’s public virtual-receptionist plans on 5 October 2026 start at **$250 a month for 50 minutes**, then **$395 / 100 minutes**, **$720 / 200 minutes**, **$1,725 / 500 minutes**. You pay for talk time, including the calls that were wrong numbers, unless the contract says otherwise. Full breakdown of that shape is in [virtual receptionist pricing](/blog/virtual-receptionist-pricing).

**Per-call live receptionist**

[Smith.ai’s live receptionist plans](/blog/smith-ai-pricing) on the same date: **Starter 30 calls for $300/month** (overage **$11.50/call**), **Basic 90 calls for $810** (overage **$10.50**), **Pro 300 calls for $2,100** (overage **$8.50**). Spam is not billed on those live plans if caller ID passes, per their FAQ. Booking, payments and extra notifications are add-ons on top.

**AI receptionist billed per call or per month**

Smith.ai’s AI receptionist on 5 October 2026: a free tier with 25 calls then **$3.00/call**, **Pro at $150/month** with 75 calls at **$2.00/call**, **Enterprise at $500/month** with 300 calls at **$1.67/call**. Other AI receptionists publish a flat monthly with included minutes and an overage rule. See [AI receptionist pricing](/blog/ai-receptionist-pricing) for that grid.

Pick the shape first. Per-user plus an AI add-on is a phone system with a receptionist bolted on. Per-call and per-minute are receptionists. Flat AI is a receptionist with a ceiling. Mixing a $15 Nextiva screenshot with a $300 Smith.ai plan is how people get a spreadsheet that does not survive the first invoice.

## What to ask before you sign

:::steps
Annual or monthly? :: Have them print both. Core’s $15 is the annual number. Engage’s $25 is too. Monthly is $23 and $50.
How many seats, really? :: Count every person who needs the app, not the number of vans. Three people is three lines.
What is the XBert number on this account? :: “From $99” is marketing. You want included volume, overage, and whether after-hours is the same rate.
What else hits the invoice? :: Extra numbers, porting, desk phones, texting registration, taxes. If it is not on the quote, it can still show up.
Who answers if XBert cannot? :: A transfer to your cell is not the same as a trained operator. Get the path in one sentence.
How do I cancel, and what happens to the number? :: Nextiva says cancel anytime on the page. Confirm port-out, notice period, and whether annual is prepaid.
:::

Do not skip the XBert question. A Core quote without it is a phone bill. The thing you woke up to price-check was the answered call.

It is 2:40 on a Wednesday afternoon, and a landscaper is standing behind a mower on a client's back lawn. His phone buzzes in the truck. It is a new customer asking for a quote on a retaining wall, and the call rolls to voicemail. The customer does not leave a message. They call the next name on the list.

He set up a Grasshopper number years ago so customers would not have his personal cell. It still works. It also does not answer the phone while he is on the mower, and that is the gap that sends people searching for an alternative.

This guide prices the main options from each vendor's own page, fetched on 14 October 2026, and tells you who each one suits.

## The short answer

If you want a cheap business number and you answer your own calls, Google Voice ($0 to $10 a month for an individual) or Quo (from $15 to $19 a month per user) will do what Grasshopper does for about the same money.

If the problem is calls you miss while working, you need something that answers. That means either an AI receptionist (free to about $200 a month depending on vendor) or a live answering service ($250 to $300 a month at the entry tier).

If you run a team that shares one number, Quo and Nextiva are built for it.

## What Grasshopper costs now

Grasshopper's pricing page advertises plans starting at $14 a month, with annual billing saving over 20%. It offers a 7-day free trial with no credit card. The page says over 400,000 customers have used it.

The per-plan breakdown loads dynamically, so I could not read the individual tier prices in the page I fetched. Open the page yourself and write down the price of the tier you would buy, along with its minutes and number limits. Every comparison below depends on that number.

The FAQ headings on the page cover extensions, extra numbers, contracts, international calling, and professional greeting recordings. That tells you the product is a virtual phone system built around forwarding and greetings. I did not find an AI receptionist described in the part of the page I could read. If one has been added since, factor it in.

## Why people look for an alternative

Four reasons come up most.

**Nobody answers while you work.** A forwarded line rings your phone. If your hands are full, the call goes to voicemail, and voicemail loses jobs.

**Texting.** Customers text. If you want a shared inbox where more than one person can reply, you need a platform built around messages.

**Team features.** One owner and one helper is a different problem from one owner alone. Ring groups, schedules and a shared inbox matter once a second person touches the phone.

**Price against what you use.** If you are paying for features you do not touch, a cheaper plan elsewhere is fair game.

Those four reasons point to different products. Pick yours before you shop.

## The main alternatives, priced

None of the prices below include NexPhone. Our own product comes after this list, in its own section.

### Quo (formerly OpenPhone)

Quo is a business phone app for calls and texts, aimed at small teams.

- **Starter.** $19 per user per month, or $15 on yearly billing. One new or ported number per user, calling and texting to US and Canadian numbers, voicemail transcripts.
- **Business.** $33 per user per month, or $23 yearly. Adds AI call summaries, phone menus, call transfers, group calling, auto call recording and analytics.
- **Scale.** $47 per user per month, or $35 yearly. Adds AI call tags, onboarding support, and inbound phone support.
- **AI.** Every plan includes its Sona AI agent with 1,000 free credits. One call is 100 credits, so that is about 10 calls. More credits run from $25 a month (400 calls' worth listed as 4,000 credits) up to $199 a month.
- **Texting registration.** $19.50 one-time plus $1.50 to $3 a month, charged by the carrier registry.
- **Porting.** Quo says it will help port a number in for free.

Good for owners who want a clean app and a shared inbox. The AI answering is a metered extra, so price it against your call volume. We go deeper in [Quo pricing](/blog/quo-pricing) and [Quo alternatives](/blog/quo-alternatives).

### Google Voice

Google Voice is the low-cost route if you already live in Google's tools.

- **Basic.** $0. One number across your devices, unlimited domestic calling and texting, but it needs an existing mobile number.
- **Starter.** $10 a month. Adds 24/7 Google support, call transfers, three-way calling, call recording and desk phone support.
- **Standard.** $20 a month, with a 50% discount for the first 6 months listed on the page. Adds auto attendants and Gemini call notes.
- **Team plans.** $10, $20 and $30 per user per month, and they require a Google Workspace plan billed separately.

Good for a solo owner who wants a second line and does not need an AI to answer. Our [Google Voice alternative](/blog/google-voice-alternative) and [Grasshopper vs Google Voice](/blog/grasshopper-vs-google-voice) posts cover it in more detail.

### Nextiva

Nextiva sells a full business phone platform with an AI receptionist called XBert. Its page says XBert answers calls, books appointments, qualifies leads and transfers calls to a person with context. Pricing is by quote on that page ("Get My Quote"), so I will not give you a number. Our [Nextiva pricing](/blog/nextiva-pricing) guide has what we could confirm.

Good for a team that wants a bigger platform and will talk to a sales rep. Heavy for a one-truck shop.

### Ruby (live receptionists)

Ruby's live-answering plans are billed by receptionist minutes.

- **50 minutes a month.** $250
- **100 minutes a month.** $395
- **200 minutes a month.** $720
- **500 minutes a month.** $1,725

Ruby lists payment collection, scheduling, lead intake and 24/7 live answering across plans. Good if you want a human voice on every call and can afford it.

### Smith.ai

Smith.ai sells both a human-first and an AI-first service.

- **Human receptionists.** Starter is $300 a month for 30 calls ($11.50 per call over). Basic is $810 for 90 calls. Pro is $2,100 for 300 calls.
- **AI receptionist.** A free tier covers 25 calls a month, then $3 per call. Pro is $150 a month for 75 calls at $2 each. Enterprise starts at $500 for 300 calls.
- **Add-ons.** Booking an appointment is $1.50 per call. Call recording and transcription is $0.25 per call. Accepting payments is $1.00 per call.
- **Spam.** Smith.ai says it does not charge for spam calls on the human plans.

Good if you want the option to hand a call to a live agent. See our [Smith.ai pricing](/blog/smith-ai-pricing) breakdown.

## A month of each, side by side

Say you take 40 calls a month and want every one answered. Here is the arithmetic from the vendor pages, with the caveat that per-call vendors depend on how many calls you get.

- **Grasshopper.** From $14 a month for the number. It forwards to your phone, so unanswered calls are still unanswered.
- **Google Voice Starter.** $10 a month. Same story on unanswered calls.
- **Quo Starter.** $19 a month, plus about $19.50 one-time and $1.50 to $3 a month to text. Sona covers the first 10 AI calls free, and extra credits cost more.
- **Smith.ai AI, Pro.** $150 a month covers 75 calls.
- **Smith.ai human, Starter.** $300 for 30 calls, plus $11.50 each for the other 10, so about $415.
- **Ruby, 50 minutes.** $250, and 40 calls can burn through 50 minutes quickly.

The first three give you a number. The last three give you an answered phone. Those are different purchases at very different prices.

## How to choose

:::steps
Count your missed calls :: Check your call log for a normal month. If you miss more than a handful, a forwarding number alone will not fix it.
Decide who answers :: A person, an AI, or you. This sets the price range more than any feature does.
Check texting :: If customers text you, confirm the carrier registration cost and wait time before you switch.
Port before you cancel :: Keep Grasshopper running until the new service has your number, then cancel.
Test on a real call :: Call the new line from another phone and ask the awkward question. See what it does when it does not know.
:::

## Mistakes to avoid when switching

Cancelling Grasshopper before the port completes is the classic one. Your number can go dark for days. Start the port, wait for confirmation, then cancel.

The second is buying per-call or per-minute plans on a guess. Pull a month of call history first and price against it. A plan that looks cheap on a quiet month turns expensive in your busy season.

The third is skipping the test. Call your own new line at night and listen. Whatever greets your customer is now the voice of your business. ## Which one for which owner

A solo owner who answers every call and wants a second line should look at Google Voice or Quo Starter.

A two or three person crew sharing one number should look at Quo Business or Nextiva.

An owner who loses jobs to the phone ringing at the wrong moment should look at an AI receptionist, whether that is Smith.ai's AI tier, Quo's Sona, or NexPhone.

An owner who needs a person on every call, for payments or sensitive conversations, should look at Ruby or Smith.ai's human plans and accept the price.

## The short answer

**What Quo costs:** Starter is **$19 per user per month**, Business is **$33**, Scale is **$47** on monthly billing. Pay yearly and those drop to **$15**, **$23** and **$35** per user. Those figures are from [Quo's pricing page](https://www.quo.com/pricing) as of 28 September 2026.

**What that buys:** a business phone system. Numbers, calling and texting to the US and Canada, voicemail transcripts, and Sona — Quo's AI agent — with a small credit allowance. A live receptionist does not pick up the phone for you.

**What else you pay:** every plan includes **1,000 Sona credits**. Quo counts **1 call = 100 credits**, so that is **10 AI-answered calls**. More than that is a credit pack, from **$25 to $199 a month**. US texting also needs Campaign Registry registration: **$19.50 once** and **$1.50–$3 a month**.

## OpenPhone is now Quo

If you bookmarked an OpenPhone pricing post from last year, you are in the right place. The company renamed the product Quo. The workspace, the numbers, and the per-user plans are the same kind of thing they always sold: a phone system you run from an app, with sharing across a small team.

That rename is why "openphone pricing" still pulls traffic and "quo pricing" is the page that should answer it. The prices below are Quo's, dated 28 September 2026. If a round-up still quotes OpenPhone at a different number, it is stale.

Quo is a phone system with an AI agent bolted on. It is in the same shopping trip as [Google Voice for business](/blog/google-voice-for-business) and other app-based lines, not the same trip as a staffed answering service. If you came here because you want a person to take the 11pm job call, skip to the category section and then read [what the best answering services for small business actually charge](/blog/best-answering-service-for-small-business).

## The three Quo plans

Quo sells seats. Every paid user gets one local or toll-free number. Calling and messaging to US and Canadian numbers is included, subject to Quo's fair use policy. Sona is included on all three plans. The plan you pick changes the phone-system features around that, and the support queue you wait in.

**Starter — $19 per user per month, or $15 if you pay a year up front ($180 billed annually)**

Built for getting a number in someone's pocket. You get the number, calling, messaging, voicemail transcripts, the Quo API, email ticket support, and Sona with the included 1,000 credits. Shared numbers work for up to 10 paid users. Call recording is manual. AI summaries and transcripts on Starter apply to calls Sona handled, not every call the humans took.

**Business — $33 per user per month, or $23 yearly ($276 billed annually)**

This is the plan most small teams need once they outgrow "we just need a number." Phone menus live here. So do automatic call recording, AI summaries and transcripts on all calls, group calling, custom ring orders, call transfers, analytics, HubSpot and Salesforce, and live chat support. Shared numbers are no longer capped at 10 paid users.

**Scale — $47 per user per month, or $35 yearly ($420 billed annually)**

Business, plus AI call tags, dedicated onboarding, priority live chat and email, and inbound phone support. You are paying for the queue and the onboarding, not a different phone.

Quo runs a 7-day free trial and says it will remind you before it converts. Month to month after that. Yearly is the discount, not a different product.

A feature sitting on Business is a feature you do not have on Starter. Phone menus, auto recording, and the CRM hooks are the usual reason a three-person shop leaves Starter. Do the seat maths on Business before you fall in love with $19.

## What sits on top of the seat price

The plan is the floor. Four line items show up after it.

**Extra numbers — $5 a month each.**
Each user includes one new or ported number. A second line for Google, a tracking number, a city you advertise in costs $5 a month, on every plan. Quo says porting from another carrier is free, and that the extra-number cost still applies.

**US texting registration — $19.50 once, then $1.50–$3 a month.**
Quo will not reliably deliver texts to AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon until The Campaign Registry has reviewed the business. That is a carrier rule, not a Quo upsell. The one-time review fee on Quo's page is **$19.50**. The monthly messaging fee is **$1.50 to $3**. Receiving texts and taking calls does not wait on that review. Outbound business texting does. The longer version of why this exists is in [10DLC registration](/blog/10dlc-registration).

**Sona credit packs — $0 to $199 a month.**
Covered in the next section. The included 10 AI calls are easy to miss in the "Sona included" headline.

**Automated SMS — 1¢ per segment.**
If you send texts through Quo's API, Zapier or Make, that is a separate 1¢ per segment on top of the plan.

International calling is an add-on with its own per-minute rates. Skip it if every customer is in the US or Canada.

There is no setup fee on the published plans. Taxes get added at checkout. Quo flags a fair use policy on the "unlimited" US and Canada calling and messaging. If a plan says unlimited, read that policy before you assume a heavy month is free.

## Sona, the included AI, and the 10-call allowance

Sona is Quo's AI agent. It answers calls when you cannot. Every Quo plan now includes it, with **1,000 free automation credits**. The conversion Quo prints on the pricing page:

- 1 call = 100 credits
- 1,000 credits = 10 calls

Need more, and you buy a credit tier. Same page, 28 September 2026:

- **Tier 1 — $0/month.** 1,000 credits (10 calls). Overage **$1.00** per call.
- **Tier 2 — $25/month.** 4,000 credits (40 calls). Overage **$0.75**.
- **Tier 3 — $49/month.** 10,000 credits (100 calls). Overage **$0.65**.
- **Tier 4 — $99/month.** 25,000 credits (250 calls). Overage **$0.55**.
- **Tier 5 — $199/month.** 60,000 credits (600 calls). Overage **$0.45**.

The $25 pack is the first one that looks like real after-hours coverage for a one-van shop. Forty AI-answered calls is a quiet month for some trades and a busy week for others. Model the month the ads work, not the month they do not.

Overage is the part that bites. At $1.00 a call on the included tier, a 30-call overflow month is another $20 on top of seats you already pay. The packs flatten that, at the cost of a second line item you will forget to cancel if volume drops.

Sona is an AI agent on a phone system. It is not a trained receptionist sitting on a script about your trade. If you are weighing this against products built as an [AI receptionist for small business](/blog/ai-receptionist-for-small-business), read the [AI receptionist pricing](/blog/ai-receptionist-pricing) breakdown next to this one. The billing units are different (seats and credits here, minutes or calls there), and mixing them in your head is how a $19 quote becomes a $90 bill.

On Starter, AI summaries and transcripts are for Sona-handled calls. Human-answered calls get that treatment on Business and Scale.

## What a small team actually pays

Do this on paper before you start the trial. Quo will not do the multiplication for you.

:::steps
Count the people who need the app :: Quo bills per user. Three techs with the app is three seats, even if they share one customer-facing number.
Pick the plan that has the features you need :: Phone menus, auto recording and HubSpot are on Business. Starter at $19 is the wrong column if you need those.
Decide how many calls Sona should take :: Ten are included. A $25 pack covers 40. A $49 pack covers 100.
Add numbers and texting :: Extra numbers are $5 a month. US outbound texting adds $19.50 once and $1.50–$3 a month after registration.
:::

**One person, Starter, light AI.**
$19 for the seat. One number included. Ten Sona calls included. Carrier registration when you want to text customers first. A quiet solo month lands around **$19–$22** after the monthly messaging fee, plus the one-time $19.50. If Sona is taking the evening overflow and 10 calls will not cover it, add **$25** and you are in the mid-forties.

**Three people, Starter.**
3 × $19 = **$57 a month**. Still no phone menus, no auto recording, no HubSpot. Shared numbers are allowed up to 10 paid users, so the three of you can share the customer line. Sona credits do not look like they stack per seat on the published page — the 1,000 credits are listed as included on the plan, with account-level packs on top. Budget the $25 pack if after-hours volume is real. **$82** plus numbers and 10DLC is a normal 3-person Starter shape.

**Three people, Business.**
3 × $33 = **$99 a month**. This is the first plan with phone menus and automatic recording. Yearly billing is 3 × $23 = **$69 a month**, billed at $276 per user per year. Add Sona's $25 or $49 pack on the months you want the AI on more than 10 calls. A working figure for a 3-person Business workspace with modest AI is **$124–$148 a month** before extra numbers.

**Five people, Business, 100 AI calls.**
5 × $33 = **$165**, plus Sona Tier 3 at **$49**, is **$214 a month** before extra numbers and 10DLC. Scale at 5 × $47 = **$235** before Sona, if inbound phone support is the reason you are there.

Porting an existing number in is listed as free. Getting a number at all, and deciding whether to port or start fresh, is a separate job from the plan math — [how to get a business phone number](/blog/how-to-get-a-business-phone-number) covers that path.

The bill inside Quo is "how many heads" times "which column has the menus" times "how many calls the AI will take." Miss any one of those and the $19 you saw in the ad is not the invoice.

## Six things to check before you pay

1. **Who needs a seat?** Anyone who has to place a call, send a text, or see the shared inbox from the app is a user. A person who only takes an occasional forwarded ring on their cell may not. Per-user billing punishes "give everyone the app just in case."
2. **Do you need a phone menu?** If yes, you are on Business. Starter will not grow a menu later without a plan change.
3. **Who answers at 8pm — a person or Sona?** If it is Sona, 10 included calls is the starter allowance, not a coverage plan. Price the credit pack against a busy week, not an average one.
4. **When can you text customers?** After Campaign Registry approval. Calls and inbound texts are the day-one path. Outbound business SMS waits on that review.
5. **How many numbers do you actually run?** Van, Google listing, a second town, a tracking number for ads. Each extra is $5 a month.
6. **What does fair use mean on "unlimited" calling?** Quo stars it. Read the policy. Heavy outbound months are where those asterisks show up.

## Quo vs a receptionist vs an answering service

Quo is cheap per month next to a staffed front desk because it is not a staffed front desk. Keep the categories straight or the prices lie.

**A business phone system (Quo, and others like it).**
You buy numbers, calling, texting, and an app the team shares. AI may answer some of the calls, metered in credits or minutes. A human employee still has to exist for anything the AI should not touch. Published Quo seats on 28 September 2026 start at $19 per user.

**A virtual receptionist.**
A person answers as your shop, from a script, and you pay per minute or per call. [Ruby](https://www.ruby.com/plans-and-pricing/) published virtual receptionist plans from **$250 a month for 50 minutes** up to **$1,725 for 500 minutes** (28 September 2026). [Smith.ai's human receptionist](https://smith.ai/pricing/receptionists) published **$300 a month for 30 calls**, then $810 for 90 and $2,100 for 300, with overage from $8.50 to $11.50 a call (same date). That is a different product. You are buying labour. The per-month number is supposed to look large next to $19.

**An AI receptionist, billed as the receptionist.**
The product is the voice that answers, qualifies the job, and passes it to you. Billing is usually a flat month or a call bundle, not a seat. Smith.ai's AI receptionist published a free tier at 25 calls, then **$150 a month at $2.00/call** on Pro (28 September 2026). Wider context for that model is on [virtual receptionist pricing](/blog/virtual-receptionist-pricing) and [AI receptionist pricing](/blog/ai-receptionist-pricing).

Use Quo when the thing you need is a shared business line the crew can call and text from, with some AI on the overflow. Use a receptionist when a human has to take a card, run a long intake, or sit inside a CRM you already pay for. Mixing the two in one quote is how people think they bought after-hours coverage and actually bought a cheaper phone app.

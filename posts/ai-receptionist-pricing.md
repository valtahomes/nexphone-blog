## The short answer

**AI receptionist pricing in 2026 comes in two published shapes.** Flat monthly plans run roughly **$20 to $200** and do not grow with volume. Per-call AI plans start free or at **$150 a month** and then charge about **$1.67 to $3.00 per answered call**. That is how a cheap-looking AI line becomes a $300 invoice in a busy month.

**A live answering service still costs more at the same volume.** [Ruby](https://www.ruby.com/plans-and-pricing/) published a **$250 / month** plan for 50 receptionist minutes on 25 August 2026. [Smith.ai's live receptionist](https://smith.ai/pricing/receptionists) Starter plan was **$300 / month for 30 calls** the same day, with overage at **$11.50 a call**.

**The number that matters is the month, not the headline.** Price 80 of your actual inbound calls, including the wrong numbers, after overage. That figure is what you compare to one missed job.

## Why the prices look nothing like each other

"AI receptionist" is a category label, not a billing model. Some products are a voice on top of a business phone line. Others are a call centre with an AI layer. A third type is a full virtual front desk that books a calendar and takes a card. They publish prices that cannot be lined up until you know what you are buying.

The category itself is the one growing. Searches for [AI receptionist for small business](/blog/ai-receptionist-for-small-business) are the queries moving, not the older "answering service" phrasing. Pricing pages have not settled on one shape yet, which is why a Sunday-night comparison feels like three different products.

A live service is still the right buy for some owners. It is a different invoice. Treat it as a different invoice.

## The three ways an AI receptionist charges

### Flat monthly

You pay one number. The AI answers, the invoice stays put, a busy July costs the same as a quiet January.

- **Shape:** roughly **$20–$200 a month**
- **You pay more when:** almost never, which is the point
- **Watch:** a minutes cap on the AI itself, and what happens when you hit it. "Unlimited" almost always has a fair-use clause. Find it before the month you need the capacity.

### Per answered call

A monthly base, then a rate for each call the AI picks up.

- **Shape, 25 August 2026:** Smith.ai's AI Receptionist [publishes](https://smith.ai/pricing/ai-receptionist) a free tier at **$0 / month** with 25 calls included and **$3.00** after that. Pro is **$150 / month** at **$2.00 a call** for about 2–3 calls a day (75 calls on the card). Enterprise is **$500 / month** at **$1.67 a call**. Extra calls on Pro are listed at **$2.50**.
- **You pay more when:** volume climbs. That is also when you have the least time to audit the bill.
- **Watch:** whether a hang-up, a robocall, or a 20-second wrong number counts as a "call." Smith.ai says known spam is filtered before the AI answers and does not count, and AI customers get an allowance to remove up to 10 percent of calls as spam each cycle. Ask any other vendor the same question in writing.

### Per minute

Less common on AI-first pages, standard on live answering. You buy a bucket of talk time.

- **Shape:** a base fee plus roughly **$1.00–$2.00 a minute** on older live plans. The industry-wide version of this maths lives in [what an answering service costs](/blog/answering-service-cost).
- **You pay more when:** calls run long, or you get a lot of them
- **Watch:** hold time, rounding up to the full minute, and supplier calls billed as customer minutes

A fourth option sits next to these and is easy to confuse with them: **hiring**. That is a wage, a shift, and a person who goes home. It is the right move if you also need a body at a desk. It is a different purchase from "answer the line at 11pm."

:::steps
Count last month's inbound calls :: Use the phone bill or the call log, not a guess. Split after-hours from daytime if you can.
Write the average talk time :: Three minutes is a decent starting point for a trade call that takes an address and a problem. Longer if you want a full intake.
Apply each vendor's unit :: Calls × their per-call rate, or minutes × their per-minute rate, or the flat month. Add published overage.
Add the extras you will actually use :: Extra numbers, extra seats, booking add-ons, payment add-ons, carrier text registration. Skip the ones you will not turn on.
:::

Do that on paper for a *busy* month. Average months make per-call and per-minute plans look cheaper than they behave in July.

## The line items people miss

**Overage.** A $150 plan is a $150 plan until call 76. Smith.ai prints the extra-call rate. Plenty of vendors do not. If it is missing, ask, and get the answer in the quote.

**What counts as a billable call.** A 12-second robocall, a hang-up on ring two, a parts supplier, a caller who has the wrong company. Live Smith.ai does not bill spam. Smith.ai AI counts answered calls and then lets you strip some of them. Ruby bills minutes, so a long wrong number is just minutes. These rules move the month more than the headline rate.

**The AI minutes cap, on flat plans.** A $20 phone plan that includes 30 AI minutes is not 30 hours of coverage. It is 30 minutes of the AI talking. At 2 minutes a call, that is about 15 answered conversations, then the overflow rule kicks in. Read the overflow rule. Some products hand the next call to voicemail. Some hand it to you. Some want you to buy a larger AI bucket.

**After-hours, priced the same or not.** Smith.ai's live page says 2pm and 2am cost the same. That is not universal. A live service that uses a cheaper overflow team at night is two products sold as one. For a plumber, the night call is the one you were buying. See [answering service for plumbers](/blog/answering-service-for-plumbers).

**Texting.** Receiving a text and taking a call usually work as soon as the number is live. Sending business texts in the US needs a one-time carrier registration (A2P 10DLC). That is a carrier rule. It takes a few business days. Anyone who tells you outbound texting is instant on day one is skipping that step.

**Lock-ins.** Smith.ai AI is month to month with 30 days' notice. Smith.ai live is month to month. Annual prepay sometimes buys 10 percent. Read the cancellation line. Answering-service contracts with a 12-month tail are still common in the live half of this market.

**Setup.** Ruby and Smith.ai both say no setup fee on the pages above. "No setup fee" is not the same as "live in an hour." Scripts, greetings, and forwarding still take a sitting.

## Six questions before you pay

1. **"What does one of my months cost at 80 calls and at 120?"** If they can only quote the headline, you do not have a quote.
2. **"What happens when I hit the cap?"** Voicemail, a human, an automatic upgrade, or a surprise overage. Four different products.
3. **"Do you bill hang-ups, robocalls, and wrong numbers?"** Get this in writing on per-call and per-minute plans.
4. **"Who answers at 11pm, and is it the same thing that answers at 10am?"** For live services this is a staffing question. For AI it is a model-and-escalation question. Same test.
5. **"What does it do when it does not know the answer?"** The only acceptable answer is that it stops and hands off to a person. An AI that fills silence with a guess costs more than voicemail.
6. **"Which of these do I actually get: a booked calendar slot, a card taken, a Jobber or ServiceTitan entry, a texted lead with a transcript?"** Three of those four require a live service or a specialist tool. Do not pay AI prices expecting a live-service job, or live-service prices for a texted lead.

Question 6 is where people buy the wrong category. An AI that answers, qualifies, and passes you the job is a different purchase from a receptionist who books Thursday at 2pm while the caller is still talking.

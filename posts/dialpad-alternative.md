It is 4:37 on a Thursday. A two-person electrical shop has Dialpad open on a laptop in the van. The owner is trying to work out why this month's AI Agent credits vanished on a run of robocalls, and a homeowner with a dead panel is calling the number on the Google listing. The call hits a flow nobody finished configuring. Four rings. Voicemail. The homeowner dials the next electrician on the page.

That is the usual reason someone types **dialpad alternative** into Google. The product is bigger than the business holding the phone.

## The short answer

**What people mean by a Dialpad alternative:** a simpler business phone a two-to-ten person shop can run without a dedicated admin. A real US number, shared calls and texts, and something that picks up when the cell is in a pocket you cannot reach.

**What it costs, as of 30 September 2026:** Quo is **$19–$47 per user per month** on monthly billing. Grasshopper starts at **$14 a month**. A live receptionist starts around **$250–$300 a month** at the low end (Ruby 50 minutes at $250, Smith.ai 30 calls at $300). Dialpad does not publish a comparable monthly sticker price on the public page.

**What you are choosing between:** a lighter team phone app, another full phone platform, or a receptionist — human or AI — sitting on the inbound line. Those are different products. Switching from Dialpad into the wrong one is how you pay twice.

**What to judge the switch on:** who answers at 11pm, what a busy month costs once overages and per-user seats are in, and whether the number on the van can move with you.

## Why this search happens

Dialpad sells an AI communications platform. AI Agents, Support, Sell, Connect. Conversation-based pricing for the agent layer. You buy credits, and a conversation is billable when the AI retrieves information or takes an action such as scheduling or routing. That is a serious product for a team that lives on the phone all day.

A lot of the people on this query never needed that stack. They needed a number that did not ring their personal cell, a way for two people to share it, and a shot at catching the inbound job while they were on a ladder. They landed in Dialpad because the ads said AI, and then spent the next three months inside admin screens.

Three patterns show up again and again.

**The bill is a function of seats and credits.** Per-user phone platforms get more expensive as you add the apprentice, the office person, the seasonal helper. AI credits add a second meter. A quiet month looks fine. A good month — the one you are too busy to audit — is when both meters run.

**The AI is a platform feature, not a receptionist.** Dialpad's agent model is built to work across voice and digital channels, with handoffs into a human team. That is the right design for a support desk. It is a heavy design for a plumber who wants the 11pm leak call qualified and texted over.

**Nobody owns the admin.** In a ten-person office, someone lives in the phone system. In a two-van shop, that someone is also the estimator, the tech, and the person who has to call the supply house back. A product that needs a dedicated owner will quietly degrade into "we forward everything to the cell and hope."

If the pain is missed inbound jobs, you may be shopping in the wrong aisle. The [best answering services for small business](/blog/best-answering-service-for-small-business) comparison is built around one month of calls, which is a different question from "which UCaaS has more integrations."

## The four shapes of Dialpad alternative

Ignore brand for a minute. Every option on this page is one of four jobs.

### 1. A simpler team phone

Same idea as Dialpad, calling, texting, a shared number, with less contact-center around it. Quo is the one most people mean.

**Good at:** a shared inbox, ringing two or three people, keeping the business number off your personal SIM.
**Watch:** per-user pricing still grows with headcount. The included AI agent is metered in credits. On Quo, as of 30 September 2026, every plan includes 1,000 automation credits, which they describe as 10 calls. Extra credit packs start at $25 a month for 4,000 credits (40 calls) and go up to $199 a month for 60,000 credits (600 calls), with overage from $1.00 down to $0.45 per call depending on the pack.

Quo's monthly list prices on that date:

- Starter — **$19 per user per month**
- Business — **$33 per user per month**
- Scale — **$47 per user per month**

Yearly billing drops those to $15, $23 and $35 per user. Extra numbers are $5 a month. Carrier registration for US texting is a $19.50 one-time Campaign Registry fee plus $1.50–$3 a month. The full breakdown lives on [Quo pricing](/blog/quo-pricing).

Starter is a phone. Business adds the menus, group calling, auto-recording and HubSpot/Salesforce connectors a lot of Dialpad leavers were using. Buy the plan that matches the job, not the one that looks cheapest on a one-user spreadsheet.

### 2. A virtual number that rings your cell

Grasshopper is the old version of this: a professional number with menus and extensions, pointed at the phones you already carry.

**Good at:** looking like a business for $14 a month (Grasshopper's public starting price on 30 September 2026) and a 7-day trial with no card.
**Watch:** you are still the person answering. A virtual number does not take a message at 11pm, qualify a leak, or text you the address. It gives the caller a business caller ID and a menu. If missed calls are the problem, a cheaper number does not fix it.

[Google Voice for business](/blog/google-voice-for-business) sits in this same bucket for a lot of one-person shops. Cheap, familiar, and easy to outgrow the moment you need a second user, a real after-hours answer, or texts that deliver to US carriers.

### 3. Another full phone platform

Nextiva is the common "we still want a proper phone system" move. XBert is their AI receptionist: greet, book, capture the lead, hand off to a human with context. The public product page on 30 September 2026 did not list a price. Sales number on the page is 800-799-0600, with a 30-day money-back claim.

**Good at:** staying inside a full business-phone world — calling, texting, meetings, a unified inbox — if that is genuinely what you run.
**Watch:** you can leave Dialpad and recreate the same admin burden under a different logo. If the complaint was "this is too much product," try a smaller product, not a lateral move. More on that swap in [Nextiva alternative](/blog/nextiva-alternative).

### 4. A receptionist on the line

This is the option Dialpad shoppers skip, and it is often the one that matches the original pain.

A live virtual receptionist (Ruby, Smith.ai's human-first plans) answers as your front desk, takes intake, and can collect a card or type into a CRM while the caller is still talking. An [AI receptionist for small business](/blog/ai-receptionist-for-small-business) answers on the first ring, holds a conversation from information you gave it, and passes the job to you.

**Good at:** the call that arrives while both hands are busy. That is the call Dialpad was supposed to save, and the one a simpler phone app still dumps on you.
**Watch:** you are buying answering, not a contact center. Live plans bill by the minute or by the call. AI plans bill by the month or by the call. Price them against your inbound volume, not against Dialpad's feature list.

Ruby, fetched 30 September 2026:

- 50 minutes — **$250 / month**
- 100 minutes — **$395 / month**
- 200 minutes — **$720 / month**
- 500 minutes — **$1,725 / month**

Same feature set on every tier. 24/7 live answering is in the plan, including weekends and holidays. Payment collection and scheduling are on the feature list. Minutes are the thing you run out of.

Smith.ai live receptionists, same date:

- Starter — **$300 / month** for 30 calls, overage **$11.50 / call**
- Basic — **$810 / month** for 90 calls, overage **$10.50 / call**
- Pro — **$2,100 / month** for 300 calls, overage **$8.50 / call**

Month to month, 30-day money-back on the initial plan up to $1,000, no charge for spam if caller ID passes. Booking a calendar is $1.50 extra per call. Taking a payment is $1.00 extra. Recording and transcription is $0.25 extra.

Smith.ai's AI receptionist, same date:

- Free — **$0 / month**, 25 calls, then **$3.00 / call**
- Pro — **$150 / month**, 75 calls at **$2.00 / call**
- Enterprise — **$500 / month**, 300 calls at **$1.67 / call**

Known spam is filtered before it counts. Other answered calls count toward the quota, with up to 10% removable each cycle. Human backup is part of the pitch. The AI can transfer into Smith.ai's live agents. That is a different product from a phone system, and a different bill.

## What a month costs

Headline rates lie in different directions. Model the month you are too busy to think about the phone bill.

**Per-user phone apps.** Two techs and an office person on Quo Business is 3 × $33 = **$99 / month** before numbers, registration and Sona credit packs. Add a fourth person over summer and the bill moves with them. This looks cheap next to a live receptionist until the AI calls are no longer covered by the included 10.

**Per-minute live answering.** Ruby at $250 for 50 minutes is $5 a minute if you use the bundle exactly. A four-minute intake plus hold time is a real slice. Wrong numbers and sales calls spend the same minutes as a job. Ask how they round, and what happens at minute 51.

**Per-call live answering.** Smith.ai Starter at $300 for 30 calls is $10 a call inside the bundle, $11.50 after that. Thirty inbound customer calls is a quiet month for a shop that advertises. Spam is not billed, which is the part of their model worth copying onto your spreadsheet.

**Per-call AI.** Smith.ai AI Pro at $150 plus $2 a call over 75 is a different curve. A 120-call month is $150 + (45 × $2) = **$240**. The free tier is a trial with a $3 overage, not a plan.

**Flat phone + AI minutes.** Some products sell a business line with a pool of AI-answering minutes that does not grow when you have a good week. That is the shape to compare against per-call overage, not against Dialpad's credit story.

Run one number before you take a sales call: what is one missed inbound job worth this month. For a lot of home-service owners that number is a few hundred dollars. The answering bill has to beat it. The phone-system bill has to beat it too, or you are paying for software while the jobs still hit voicemail.

## What moves with you, and what does not

**The number can move.** Port it. The usual path is to stand up the new system, forward Dialpad at the carrier or inside Dialpad so you can test, then port when you trust the new setup. Forwarding carries calls. It does not carry texts. If customers text the van number, plan the port around that, not around voice.

**Texts need a carrier registration.** Outbound business SMS in the US goes through A2P 10DLC. That is a carrier rule. On Quo the Campaign Registry fee is $19.50 one-time plus $1.50–$3 a month. Other providers pass through something in the same neighbourhood. Receiving texts and taking calls can work on day one. Sending texts to customers is what waits on registration, a few business days, sometimes longer. The mechanics are in [10DLC registration](/blog/10dlc-registration).

**Call recordings and transcripts stay behind unless you export them.** Download what you need before you cancel. Do not assume the old host will keep a nice archive for you.

**Integrations are the trap.** If Dialpad was the front door into a CRM, a helpdesk, or a dialer, write down the three workflows you use. A simpler phone will do two of them and drop the third. A receptionist product may text you the lead and stop there. That can be fine. It is not fine if you find out on week two.

**Missed-call behaviour is not a given.** Some systems send the caller to voicemail. Some fire a text back in a few seconds so the lead is still in their hand. If speed-to-lead is why you are leaving, read [missed call text back](/blog/missed-call-text-back) before you pick a logo.

## What to check before you port

Six questions, in the order that saves money:

:::steps
Who answers at 7pm on a Saturday? :: If the honest answer is "whoever has the app open," you have bought a phone, not coverage. If it is an AI or a live receptionist with a written escalation, you have bought the thing you were missing.
What does a 150-call month cost? :: Have them price your busy month, including overage, extra seats, extra numbers and AI credits. The starter tier is a brochure.
What happens to spam and wrong numbers? :: Per-minute and per-call plans make this real money. Smith.ai live does not bill spam when caller ID passes. Quo's Sona credits are spent on answered AI calls. Get the rule in writing.
Where does the job land after the call? :: A transcript in email, a text to the owner, a calendar booking, a CRM row. If you have to re-type it, you will lose it.
Can I keep the number on the truck? :: Yes if you port. Temporary if you only forward. Texts decide which path you are on.
What is the cancellation term? :: Quo and Smith.ai are month-to-month on the pages we fetched. Ask Dialpad what you owe through the end of the term before you start the port, so you are not paying two systems for six weeks.
:::

Do a live test before you port. Call yourself at lunch. Call yourself at 9pm. Call from a number that is not in your contacts. If the 9pm call is the reason you are leaving, that is the only test that counts.

For a shop that takes emergency work, the after-hours path matters more than the daytime demo. [After-hours answering service](/blog/after-hours-answering-service) is the same test from the other side: who is actually on the line when the owner is not.

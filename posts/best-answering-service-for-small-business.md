## What you are actually choosing

This query mixes three products.

**A live answering service.** Operators answer as your business, follow a script,
and pass you a message. Good at sounding human. Bad at knowing your trade. You
pay talk time or a per-call fee, including calls that were never jobs.

**A virtual receptionist.** Further upmarket: more authority, sometimes a named
person. Some book a calendar and take a card. After-hours is often a cheaper
overflow team. Ask who answers at 2am.

**An AI receptionist.** A voice answers from information you gave it, takes the
job details, and passes you the lead. Good at the first ring, at 3am, on a bill
that does not jump because Saturday was busy. Bad at judgement if it always
produces an answer.

Most AI products do **not** book a calendar, take a card, or type into Jobber,
Housecall Pro or ServiceTitan while the caller is on the line. If those three
are the job, buy live. If the job is "answer it, qualify it, get it to me," AI
is usually cheaper.

## The seven best answering services for small business

### 1. Ruby — best live front desk if you will pay for minutes

Ruby is the product people mean by "virtual receptionist": US-based humans, 24/7,
same feature list on every plan. The only thing that changes is the minute bundle.

On ruby.com/plans-and-pricing, 19 August 2026:

- **$250/mo — 50 minutes**
- **$395/mo — 100 minutes**
- **$720/mo — 200 minutes**
- **$1,725/mo — 500 minutes**

They say no setup fee and no after-hours surcharge. Scheduling, payment
collection, bilingual answering and lead intake sit on every plan.

**Good at:** a polished first impression, booking, taking a card, nights at the
same rate as Tuesday morning.
**Bad at:** the minute maths. Fifty minutes is not a trade month. On the $250
plan you are paying **$5 a minute**. They do not publish overage, so 250 minutes
does not fit the $720 plan and the next published step is $1,725.

Buy Ruby if a human voice *and* the extra jobs (calendar, card) are the product.
It is not cheap overflow.

### 2. Smith.ai — best if you want to pay per call, live or AI

Smith.ai sells two products on two pricing pages. Live receptionists at
smith.ai/pricing/receptionists. AI at smith.ai/pricing/ai-receptionist. Both
checked 19 August 2026.

**Live receptionist**

- Starter: **$300/mo for 30 calls**, overage **$11.50/call**
- Basic: **$810/mo for 90 calls**, overage **$10.50/call**
- Pro: **$2,100/mo for 300 calls**, overage **$8.50/call**

No setup fee. Month to month. 10% off if you prepay a year. They do not count
spam or wrong numbers against the quota if caller ID is passed through — ask
them to put that in the order form. Booking a calendar is **$1.50/call**. Taking
a payment is **$1.00/call**. Recording and transcription is **$0.25/call**.

**AI receptionist**

- Free: **25 calls**, then **$3.00/call**
- Pro: **$150/mo**, **$2.00/call** at the 75-call tier, extras **$2.50**
- Enterprise: **$500/mo**, **$1.67/call** at the 300-call tier

The AI can hand off to Smith's live agents. Answered AI calls count, with a 10%
spam allowance and a filter for known robocalls.

**Good at:** a bill you can forecast from a call count, CRM logging, à la carte
booking and payments, a live backup behind the AI.
**Bad at:** the live starter. Thirty calls at $300 is **$10 a call** before
overage. A good month is a $1,000 invoice.

Buy Smith if you know your call count and may add booking later. Model a busy
month, not an average one.

AnswerConnect is a 24/7 live service. On 19 August 2026 the prices were on the

- Entry: **$350/mo for 200 minutes**, **$49.99 setup**, **$2.50** per extra minute
- Growth: **$395/mo for 300 minutes**, **no setup fee**, **$1.85** per extra minute
- Standard: **$575/mo for 400 minutes**, **$49.99 setup**, **$1.85** per extra minute

**Good at:** a published overage rate, 24/7 humans, and a 300-minute plan only

**Bad at:** full-minute rounding on short calls, and a setup fee on two of three

Buy AnswerConnect if you want live people, can estimate minutes, and want

### 4. Abby Connect — best if you want live and AI on one bill

Abby is one service with three modes: human, AI, or a mix. Receptionists work

- Essential: **$329/mo — 100 Abby Minutes**
- Professional: **$599/mo — 200 Abby Minutes**

Buy Abby if a human is available *and* some volume will actually sit on AI. If

## What to ask before you sign

:::steps
Who answers at 11pm on a Sunday? :: Same team as Tuesday at 10am, or overflow. That is the call you are buying.
What happens on an emergency? :: You want the questions they ask, the threshold, and how you are reached. Vague now is vague at 11pm.
How do you bill a wrong number, a robocall, a hang-up? :: On per-call and per-minute plans this is real money. Smith live says they skip spam if caller ID passes through. That is not the default.
What is the increment? :: AnswerConnect rounds to a full minute. Ask everyone else. Full-minute rounding on short calls is how the quote and the invoice diverge.
Can they tell an emergency from a nuisance? :: Water running is a truck out now. A slow drip is Thursday. Somebody has to know the difference.
What do you actually receive after the call? :: A message you have to re-key is a message you will lose.
What is the cancellation term? :: Annual lock-ins are common. Ruby, Smith, Abby and PATLive all say month-to-month on the pages we checked. Get it in the order form.
:::

Do not skip the Sunday-night question. A service that is excellent at 10am and
outsourced at 11pm is two products sold as one.

Before you pay a second invoice, call your own line after hours from a number
that is not in your contacts. It should pick up, sound like your business, ask
the questions that decide whether this is a job, and put the details on your
phone the same night.

If you are a plumber, HVAC tech or electrician, the missed call is often the
expensive one. The plumbing walkthrough is

## Hiring a receptionist instead

The [Bureau of Labor Statistics *Occupational Outlook Handbook*](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/receptionists.htm)
(last modified 28 August 2025) puts the median receptionist wage at **$17.90 an
hour, $37,230 a year**, from May 2024 data. Most work full time. That is one
person, on one shift, in one place.

It does not cover lunch, sick days, or the Saturday the pipe bursts. A second
person for those hours is a second wage. Payroll tax sits on top; we are not
going to invent a burden rate.

Hire the person if you need a body at a desk. Do not hire them to be a 24/7
phone tree. That is the job the seven vendors above are for.

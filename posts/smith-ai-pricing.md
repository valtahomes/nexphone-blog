## The short answer

**Live receptionist** (Smith.ai's human-first plans, fetched 29 September 2026): **$300 a month** for 30 calls, **$810** for 90, **$2,100** for 300. Go over the cap and you pay **$11.50, $10.50, or $8.50** per extra call, depending on the tier. A 30-day money-back guarantee sits on all of them. Spam is not billed if the forwarded line passes Caller ID.

**AI receptionist** (same date, their AI pricing page): **$0 a month** for 25 real calls, then **$3.00** each. **Pro is $150 a month** for 75 calls at **$2.00** a call. **Enterprise is $500 a month** for 300 calls at **$1.67**. Answered calls count toward the quota. Known spam is filtered first; you can strip up to 10% of what remains each cycle.

**What actually moves the invoice** is overage, and on the live side a stack of per-call add-ons: booking at $1.50, payments at $1.00, recording at $0.25, SMS pings at $0.50. Model a busy month with the extras you would actually switch on, not the plan card.

## Smith.ai is two products

People search "smith.ai pricing" as if it were one number.

**Human-first** is a live virtual receptionist. North America-based agents answer 24/7, follow your intake questions, and can transfer, book, take payment, or push the call into a CRM. You are paying for a person on the line.

**AI-first** is a voice AI that answers, qualifies, and routes. If the call goes past what the AI can handle, Smith.ai can hand it to that same live agent network. You are paying per AI-answered call, with a human sitting behind it.

Same company, different meters. If you mix them up on a sales call, you will compare the wrong invoice to the wrong job.

The wider market — per-minute call centres, per-call live desks, flat-rate AI — is laid out in [what an answering service costs](/blog/answering-service-cost). Smith.ai lives in the per-call column on both of its products.

- 90 calls included

**Pro — $2,100 / month**
- 300 calls included
- 10 free transfer destinations
- Overage: $8.50 per call after 300

**Enterprise — custom**
- Built around volume
- Transfer destinations by agreement

Every live plan, on that page, includes lead screening, qualification and intake, a dashboard, no setup fee, and 24/7 coverage at the same per-call rate as daytime. They state no overseas agents and no charge for spam when Caller ID on the forwarded line is intact. New accounts get a 30-day money-back guarantee, with overage excluded from the refund and a cap of $1,000.

Billing is per client call, not per minute. Smith.ai's own FAQ on that page says they do it so you are not paying for a long conversation in extra minutes. The trade is the opposite of a per-minute centre: a four-minute emergency and a forty-second wrong number can cost the same once they count as a billable call. Wrong numbers and robocalls are the exception they carve out, provided Caller ID works.

Month to month. Cancel, and they bill through the end of the current cycle. Prepay 12 months and they take 10% off.

Month to month. Cancel needs 30 days' notice, tighter than the live plans.

Spam works differently than on live. Smith.ai says the system blocks more than 20 million known spam numbers before the AI answers, and those do not count. What the AI does answer counts toward the quota. You get an allowance to remove up to 10% of calls each billing cycle for leftover spam.

The AI can transfer to Smith.ai's live North America agents when a call goes past it. That human backup is the thing they use to justify a higher per-call rate than a bare AI. It is also how a "cheap AI month" becomes a live-agent month if a lot of callers ask for a person.

## What a month actually costs

Headlines lie in opposite directions. Live Starter looks cheap until you exceed 30 calls. AI Free looks free until call 26.

Take a one-truck trade that logs **80 inbound customer calls a month**. Use only the published rates from 29 September 2026, with no add-ons.

**Live Starter at 80 calls**
- $300 for the first 30
- 50 × $11.50 = $575
- **$875**

**Live Basic at 80 calls**
- 90 included, so you sit inside the cap
- **$810**

**AI Free at 80 calls**
- 25 included
- 55 × $3.00 = $165
- **$165**

**AI Pro (75-call tier) at 80 calls**
- $150 for 75
- 5 × $2.50 = $12.50
- **$162.50**

Same 80 calls. Live is $810–$875. AI is about $165. That gap is the whole product decision.

Now add the live extras a home-service owner wants. Booking on every qualified call at $1.50, say 40 of the 80, is another $60. Payments on 20 of them at $1.00 is another $20. Recording on all 80 at $0.25 is $20. You are in the mid-$900s on Basic before anyone has a complaint call that needs a manager.

Push the same shop to **120 calls**, a busy summer month:

- Live Starter: $300 + 90 × $11.50 = **$1,335**
- Live Basic: $810 + 30 × $10.50 = **$1,125**
- Live Pro at $2,100 is cheaper only if you are already near 300
- AI Pro, 75-call tier: $150 + 45 × $2.50 = **$262.50**
- AI Pro, 150-call tier: **$270**

On that last pair, staying on the 75-call plan and paying overage undercuts the 150-call plan until you cross roughly 123 calls. Read the extra-call rate before you "upgrade" out of habit.

A quiet month of **25 calls** is the other trap. Live Starter is still $300 ($12 a call). AI Free is $0. You cannot bank unused live calls; the pages above do not advertise rollover on the month-to-month live plans. Unused AI credits are a different conversation. Enterprise mentions rollover on a 6-month plan, which is a sales conversation, not a published rate.

## How Smith.ai bills, in practice

A few rules from those two pages:

**Live does not bill spam; AI bills answered calls.** If your Google listing attracts a lot of junk, that difference shows up. Confirm Caller ID on the forward, or the live spam carve-out may not apply.

**Overage is where Starter dies.** $11.50 a call after 30 means the 31st call costs more than the average of the first 30. If you are consistently at 50–70, Basic at $810 is the honest live price, not Starter at $300.

**Add-ons are per event, not per month.** Booking, payments, recordings, and pings scale with volume. A "quiet" add-on on the sales call is a second meter.

**Contracts are short, with one catch on AI.** Live is end-of-cycle. AI wants 30 days. If you are testing, that extra month of AI is part of the cost of leaving.

**24/7 is in the per-call rate on live.** They do not surcharge nights. That is unusual against old-school answering services that sell "after hours" as a different product. You still pay for the call.

**The free AI tier is a trial with a hard meter.** 25 real calls. After that it is $3.00 each, which is higher than Pro's included rate. Treat Free as a test, not a production plan, unless your line is genuinely that quiet.

## What to ask before you sign

Six questions, in the order they will change the invoice:

1. **"Which product am I buying — live, AI, or AI with live backup — and who answers at 11pm?"** Get it in writing. A blended pitch is how AI quotes turn into live invoices.
2. **"Walk me through one emergency call, including the transfer."** You want the questions they ask, the threshold for waking you, and whether that transfer is included or another event.
3. **"Do spam, robocalls, and hang-ups count?"** Ask it twice, once for each product, because the answers diverge.
4. **"What is my bill at 40, 80, and 120 customer calls, with the add-ons I just circled?"** If they will not run those three numbers, run them yourself from the pages above.
5. **"What do I receive after the call, and where does it land?"** A summary in email, a CRM row, a text, a booking. If you have to re-type it, you will lose it.
6. **"What is the cancellation term on the plan I am actually on?"** Live and AI are not the same.

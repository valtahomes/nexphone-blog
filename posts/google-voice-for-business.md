It is 2:17 on a Thursday. You are on a customer’s roof with both hands on a condenser, the unit is heavier than it looked on the invoice, and the phone in your pocket starts buzzing. The number on the van is a Google Voice line you set up last winter because it was free. The call rings through to you. You cannot pick up. It hits voicemail. The homeowner hangs up two sentences into the recording and dials the next company on Google.

You did get a business number. You did not get the call.

:::callout
**Google Voice is a phone number that follows you around.**
That is a useful thing. It is not the same thing as someone answering when you cannot. This guide is the 2026 price list, the Workspace catch, and the point at which a cheap number stops being the product you thought you bought.
:::

## The short answer

**What it costs:** as of 16 September 2026 on [Google’s Voice page](https://workspace.google.com/products/voice/), a one-person plan is **$0, $10, or $20 a month**. A team plan is **$10, $20, or $30 per user per month**, and those team plans need **Google Workspace billed separately**. Unlimited calling and texting is in the plan copy, subject to Google’s acceptable use policy.

**What you are buying:** a cloud phone number that rings your mobile, laptop, and (on paid plans) a desk phone. Voicemail gets transcribed. Spam gets blocked. Higher plans add auto attendants, ring groups, and Gemini notes on calls you actually take.

**Where it stops:** the moment nobody is free to pick up. Voice will ring you, park the caller in a menu, or take a voicemail. A homeowner with a broken heater does not leave a voicemail. They call the next name.

## What Google Voice for business is

Google sells two shapes of the same product, and the search results mash them together.

**Just for me.** One number, one Google account. It works on your phone, a laptop, and inside Gmail. Basic is free. Starter and Standard add support, recording, transfers, desk phones, and on Standard an auto attendant plus Gemini notes. You do not need Workspace.

**For my team.** Per-user pricing, up to 10 people on Starter, unlimited users on Standard and Premier. Admins get number assignment, porting, ring groups, call queues, eDiscovery, and on Premier automatic recording with transcription and BigQuery reporting. Google’s page is blunt about the extra bill: **Workspace is required, billed separately**.

People type “google voice for business” for a few different jobs, and only one of them is the product:

- A second number so the personal cell stays private
- A number that works on more than one device
- Something that looks like a company when a customer calls
- Something that answers when the owner cannot

Voice does the first three. The fourth is an [AI receptionist for small business](/blog/ai-receptionist-for-small-business), or a live answering service. Mixing them up is how a $10 line still loses Saturday jobs.

The admin tools are real. Ring groups, multi-level auto attendants, SIP Link for bringing your own carrier, HP Poly desk phones with zero-touch setup, voicemail and SMS landing in Gmail, working hours pulled from Google Calendar so off-hours calls skip straight to voicemail. That is a phone system for an office that has people at desks. A one-van trade running off a pocket is not that office.

## Google Voice pricing

This is the related search, **google voice pricing**, and the numbers below are from Google’s Voice product page on **16 September 2026**. Prices are USD. They move. Recheck before you pay.

### Individual plans (US)

**Basic — $0 a month**

- One number across your devices
- Unlimited domestic calling and texting, subject to acceptable use
- You must already have a mobile number
- No 24/7 Voice support on this tier
- The paid plans advertise that the number will not be reclaimed for inactivity. Basic does not make that promise.

**Starter — $10 a month**

- Everything in Basic
- 24/7 Google Voice support
- Number kept through inactivity
- Three-way calling, call transfers, call recording
- Desk phone and ATA support

**Standard — $20 a month** ($10 a month for the first six months, then it returns to $20; cancel any time)

- Everything in Starter
- Gemini notes on calls
- Auto attendants for call routing

### Team plans (14 countries)

Workspace is on a separate invoice. Voice does not include it.

**Starter — $10 per user per month**

- Up to 10 users
- One number per user, with call forwarding
- Unlimited domestic calling and US texting, subject to acceptable use
- 24/7 support, three-way calling, transfers, recording, desk phones

**Standard — $20 per user per month**

- Unlimited users
- Auto attendants and ring groups
- Gemini notes
- Call queuing for ring groups
- eDiscovery for calls, voicemails, and texts
- SIP Link to connect existing numbers

**Premier — $30 per user per month**

- Everything in Standard
- Unlimited international billing locations
- Automatic call recording, transcription, and AI notes
- Advanced reporting into BigQuery

Two costs sit next to the sticker.

**Workspace.** Google does not print a Workspace price on the Voice page. Treat Voice’s $10–$30 as the phone layer only. A two-person shop on Voice Standard is $40 a month for Voice, plus whatever Workspace charges for those two seats.

**“Unlimited.”** The asterisk on Google’s page points at an acceptable use policy. Fair-use caps are how every cheap unlimited phone plan stays cheap. If a month goes loud, read that policy before you assume the bill stays flat.

A solo owner who only needs a second number can live on Basic or Starter and spend less than lunch. A five-person crew on Standard is $100 a month for Voice, plus Workspace, and still nobody answers the line while the crew is on a job. Price per user is the wrong unit if the problem is a missed inbound call.

## What a small shop is trying to buy

Most owners who land on this page are trying to stop losing work.

The pattern is the same across plumbers, HVAC techs, electricians, cleaners, landscapers. The business number is on the van, the Google listing, the invoices. The owner is the technician. The phone rings while both hands are occupied. A number that follows you around is handy for outbound calls and for looking like a company. It does nothing for the inbound call you cannot take.

Three jobs get sold as one:

**A number.** Local or toll-free, rings a device you already own. Google Voice is good at this, and so are a dozen other cloud phone products. If that is the whole job, $10 is a fair price. The guide to [getting a toll-free number](/blog/toll-free-number-for-business) lives separately.

**Routing.** Auto attendants, ring groups, working hours, voicemail. Useful when two or three people share a front desk. A phone tree at 11pm is still a phone tree. Someone watching a water heater leak onto a basement floor will not press 2 for service.

**Answering.** A voice on the line that takes a name, an address, what is broken, and when they can be there, then gets that to the owner. That is an answering service or an AI receptionist. Google Voice does not do this. Gemini notes help on calls you already answered. They do not pick up for you.

If the search was really “I need the phone answered,” skip ahead to the comparison and ignore the per-user math. If the search was “I need a number that is not my personal cell,” Voice at $10 is in the right neighborhood.

## Where Google Voice stops working

The failure is quiet, which is why it lasts.

**It rings you.** That is the design. A second number that lights up the same pocket. When you can pick up, it feels like a receptionist. When you cannot, it is a second voicemail box.

**Off-hours go to voicemail on purpose.** Voice will send calls to voicemail from your Google Calendar working hours. For an office, that is polite. For a trade that makes its margin on after-hours call-outs, that setting is the job walking away. The [missed-call text-back](/blog/missed-call-text-back) problem starts the second the recording kicks in. Most callers hang up rather than leave a message, and a text a minute later is what keeps them.

**The auto attendant is a menu.** Standard and the team plans give you multi-level auto attendants. Callers press digits. That is fine for “billing, sales, or support” in a building with a receptionist. It is a hang-up on an emergency call.

**Gemini does not take the job.** Notes, transcription, spam blocking, and voicemail-to-text fire after a human was on the line, or after the caller already left. None of them ask whether the heat is out, whether the water is shut off, or whether this is a rental that needs landlord approval.

**Team pricing assumes seats.** $20 per user is cheap compared with a desk phone system from 2014. It is the wrong shape for a two-truck shop where the “users” are in crawlspaces and the inbound line is one shared number that should be answered even when both trucks are busy.

**Spam filtering is a feature, not coverage.** Voice is good at blocking junk. That does not help the real customer who called once.

None of this makes Voice a bad product. It makes it a bad fit for the job a lot of searchers are hiring it to do. A number is solved. Coverage is not.

## What to test before you keep the number

Do this on a Tuesday night, not on the sales call.

:::steps
Call your own line at 8pm :: See what the caller actually hears. A transcribed voicemail is still a voicemail. Count how long until a human sentence.
Have someone else call while you are on a job :: Pocket in the other room, hands wet. If the result is a menu or a beep, that is your Saturday.
Ask where the details go :: A voicemail you have to replay in the van is a lead you will lose. You want a text with the address and the problem.
Price a busy month :: On Voice, that is seats plus Workspace. On per-call and per-minute services, it is volume. On flat AI, it is the monthly number. Use the loud month, not the average.
Read the inactivity and fair-use lines :: Basic Voice can reclaim a quiet number. Unlimited calling is never a blank cheque.
:::

If you already have Voice and it is doing the number job, you do not have to rip it out to fix answering. Forwarding the line is how most shops add coverage without reprinting the van. Forwarding carries calls. It does not carry texts. If the same number has to text customers, that is a port, not a forward.

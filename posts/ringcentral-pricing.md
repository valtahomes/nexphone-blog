It is 8:15 on a Thursday morning. You have twenty minutes before the first job, a laptop on the passenger seat, and RingCentral's pricing page open. You came here for a number you can put on the van. What you have instead is a user slider, a promise to save 33% if you pay a year up front, and four add-ons with "starts at" prices. There is still no figure you can write on a napkin.

RingCentral sells a communications platform. The bill is seats, plus add-ons, plus the recovery fees in the footer. This guide is what their public page showed on 21 September 2026, and how to turn that into a monthly number before anyone from sales calls you back.

:::callout
**The only number that matters is the invoice, not the sticker.**
RingCentral's published page is clear on add-ons and trial rules. It is not clear on a single per-user price for the main RingEX plans. Price the stack you would actually switch on — seats, AI, SMS, taxes — or you will learn the real figure in month two.
:::

## The short answer

**What it costs:** as of **21 September 2026**, [RingCentral's plans and pricing page](https://www.ringcentral.com/office/plansandpricing.html) does **not** print a fixed per-user price for RingEX. The numbers it does print are add-ons. **AI Receptionist starts at $39.** **Conversational Intelligence starts at $60.** **Call Queues Booster is $35.** **Business SMS Booster is $25.** Annual billing is advertised at **up to 33% off**. Taxes, 911 fees, and a "Compliance and Administrative Cost Recovery Fee" sit on top.

**What you are buying:** a cloud phone system (they call the suite RingEX) with meetings, messaging, and optional contact-center tools. The AI receptionist is an extra. So is a lot of the SMS stack. A one-van shop and a 40-person office are on the same price list, which is why the per-user maths gets away from people.

**What to judge it on:** a sample invoice for *your* headcount, with add-ons and recovery fees included. If they will not send that, you do not have a price yet.

:::stats
$39 :: RingCentral AI Receptionist, starting price on ringcentral.com, 21 September 2026
$60 :: Conversational Intelligence add-on, same page, same date
33% :: maximum annual-billing discount RingCentral advertises on that page
:::

## What RingCentral sells

RingCentral is a UCaaS company. The product on the pricing page is **RingEX**, with voice, team messaging, video, fax, auto attendant, and a pile of admin tools. The FAQ on that same page (21 September 2026) says they have **over 330 integrations across 200+ companies**, including Microsoft Teams, Salesforce, HubSpot, Zendesk and ServiceNow.

That fits multi-site offices, hybrid teams, a front desk that already lives in Teams, a support queue that needs reporting. If that is you, the rest is about not under-counting the bill.

If that is not you, if you are a plumber, an HVAC tech, a cleaner, or anyone who runs the business off a cell and loses work when both hands are busy, you are looking at a platform built for a different job. The add-on list is how RingCentral now sells "the phone gets answered," and those line items are easy to miss.

The page also sells **AI Receptionist** as something that can "answer and route calls on any phone system," starting at $39. You can keep another carrier and bolt their AI on top. You can also buy the whole RingEX stack and still need that add-on, because the base plan is a phone system, not a receptionist.

## What the public page shows (21 September 2026)

I am only using numbers that were on [ringcentral.com/office/plansandpricing.html](https://www.ringcentral.com/office/plansandpricing.html) on 21 September 2026. Where a plan price is missing, it is missing. I am not filling it in from memory.

**RingEX seat prices.** Not printed as static dollar amounts on that page. There is a user-count control, a "compare all features" panel, and a prompt to talk to an expert at **(800) 574-5290**. An FAQ on the same page names **Advanced** and **Ultra** as packages with extra admin and analytics features. It does not attach a price to either one.

**Annual discount.** "Save up to 33% by paying annually." That is a ceiling, not a guarantee that your cart will be 33% cheaper.

**Add-ons printed on the page**

- AI Receptionist — answers and routes calls, including on another phone system. **Starts at $39.**
- Conversational Intelligence — AI insights on calls. **Starts at $60.**
- Call Queues Booster — routing, insights, callbacks. **$35.**
- Business SMS Booster — shared inbox, templates, compliance tools. **$25.**

The page does not say whether those add-on figures are per user, per account, or the bottom rung of a ladder. "Starts at" is doing a lot of work. Treat $39 and $60 as floors and get the line item in writing.

**Calling.** Unlimited VoIP and conference calls inside the organisation. Unlimited external calls **within the US and Canada**. International calls bill from their published rate table. Extra toll-free minutes are sold separately. Unlimited calling is "limited to US and CA markets only" — their wording, in the disclaimers.

**SMS.** Not in the free trial. TCR registration is required for SMS/MMS, with **additional TCR fees**. Automated or high-volume SMS is a separate product (RingCentral High Volume SMS). If texting customers is half the reason you want a business number, this is not a footnote.

**Hardware.** Phone rental is available **only with a multi-year contract**. Professional implementation is listed for **two users and above**.

**Mobile User extensions.** The disclaimer is specific. Adding a Mobile User extension is free, but if that extension is used to make or receive calls in a given month, they charge it that month at the same rate as a Digital Line. A "free" extra seat that places one call is not free.

None of that is hidden. It is below the fold.

## The trial, in the terms they published

New subscribers only. **14 days.** Support during the trial is described as covering up to 20 phone lines in one FAQ answer, and the legal disclaimers on the same page cap the trial at **five users and two desktop phones**. Believe the tighter cap.

The RingCentral app (Windows, Mac, Android, iOS) is included. Auto attendant, online meetings, team messaging, fax and conferencing are included. **SMS is not.** Trial desk phones have to come back within **21 days of cancelling** or they bill you for the hardware.

A trial that cannot send a text is a useful demo of calling. It is a poor demo of the product you will run in month two if your customers live on SMS. Test the thing you will use, on a real inbound job call, before the clock runs out.

## Fees that never make the big number

The same page tells you, in the disclaimers, that other charges may apply:

- Federal Universal Service Recovery Fee
- E911 Service Fee
- Compliance and Administrative Cost Recovery Fee
- 911 Fee
- State and local taxes

You cannot decline them. They are how US phone bills are built. The trap is comparing a RingCentral "from" price to a competitor's "from" price and forgetting that both invoices grow a tail.

SMS has a second tail. TCR (The Campaign Registry) is a carrier rule, not a RingCentral invention. Their page flags TCR fees on top of the Business SMS Booster. Budget for registration and a monthly campaign fee, and assume outbound texting is not instant on day one with any US carrier.

If you want a clean comparison, ask sales for a **sample invoice for 1 user and for 3 users**, with AI Receptionist on, SMS on, and every recovery fee listed. A company that can do 330 integrations can produce a PDF.

## How to estimate a real monthly bill

Do this on paper before the demo. Fifteen minutes.

:::steps
Count the people who need a live line :: RingCentral bills by user. A helper who never answers the phone is not a seat. A second person who takes overflow after 5pm is. Mobile User extensions that place calls get billed as lines — see their own disclaimer.
Add only the add-ons you would switch on in week one :: AI Receptionist from $39, Conversational Intelligence from $60, Call Queues Booster at $35, Business SMS Booster at $25, all from the 21 September 2026 page. If you do not need queues, do not let the demo include them "for now."
Ask for taxes and recovery fees as line items :: Universal Service, E911, the compliance recovery fee, 911, state tax. If the quote is still a round number, it is not a quote.
Price annual and monthly as two different products :: Up to 33% off is the advertised annual ceiling. A year of lock-in is only a discount if you still want this stack in month 13.
Run one inbound test during the trial :: A real customer-shaped call, after hours, with the AI add-on on if you plan to buy it. SMS will not be in the trial, so do not pretend that part was proven.
:::

A one-person trade business that wants a number, voicemail, and the phone answered at night is often looking at seat plus AI plus SMS plus fees. Until RingCentral prints the seat price next to your user count, that sum has a hole in it. Fill the hole or do not sign.

## When RingCentral is the right buy

Buy it when the phone system *is* the product.

You have several people who need their own extension. You want video meetings and team chat in the same app. The stack needs Microsoft Teams calling, a contact center, or one of those 330 integrations to fire when a call ends. Someone on staff can own the admin, including call queues, roles, the SMS registration, and the hardware return window.

In that shop, the missing sticker price is annoying, not disqualifying. You get a quote, you count seats, you add AI if the front desk cannot pick up, and you pay the recovery fees like every other UCaaS bill.

It is a weaker fit when the business is one or two people in a van, the "office" is a cell phone, and the failure mode is an emergency call at 11pm while you are on a roof. You can make RingCentral do that job. You will pay for a platform, then an AI add-on, then SMS, to cover a problem that is really "nobody can pick up." For that problem, look at [AI receptionist options for small business](/blog/ai-receptionist-for-small-business) and [the best answering services for small business](/blog/best-answering-service-for-small-business) before you sign a per-user stack.

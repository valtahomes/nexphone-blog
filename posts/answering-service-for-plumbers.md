It is 11:04 on a Tuesday night. A supply line let go under a kitchen sink in a house across town, the
homeowner is standing in an inch of water with their phone in one hand and a towel in the other, and
they are calling every plumber on the first page of Google until somebody picks up.

They will not leave a voicemail. They will not call back in the morning. The plumber who answers that
call gets a job worth several hundred dollars and probably a customer for the next decade.

An answering service exists so that plumber is you.

:::callout
This guide is about the only test that matters.
Any answering service can take a message during business hours. The question is what happens on the
emergency call at 11pm — whether it gets triaged, whether it reaches you, and what it costs you per
month to make sure it does.
:::

## The short answer

**What it costs:** most plumbing answering services land between **$200 and $600 a month** on a
per-minute plan, roughly **$2–$5 per call** on a per-call plan, or **$20–$200 a month flat** for an AI
answering service that doesn't bill by volume. Prices move, so treat those as shapes rather than
quotes — and always price it against *your* call volume, not the headline rate.

**What you're actually choosing between:** a live call centre that takes messages, a virtual
receptionist who works from your script, or an AI receptionist that answers instantly and passes the
job to you. They fail in different ways, and the right answer depends on how many after-hours calls
you get and how much of your margin a missed one costs.

**What to judge it on:** not the greeting. The escalation. What happens between the caller saying
"there's water everywhere" and your phone buzzing.

## Why plumbing is a worst case for missed calls

Most businesses lose a missed call and lose a customer. Plumbing loses a missed call and loses an
*emergency* customer, which is a different economic event.

Three things make the trade unusually punishing:

**The calls arrive when you can't take them.** You are under a sink, in a crawlspace, on a ladder, or
your hands are wet and covered in pipe dope. The phone is in a pocket you can't reach. The window in
which the caller is still deciding who to hire is measured in minutes.

**The urgent ones are worth the most.** A burst line at midnight is a premium call-out. A dripping tap
next Thursday is not. The calls hardest to answer are the ones with the highest ticket.

**Callers have zero patience for a menu.** Somebody watching water spread across their floor does not
want to press 1 for service. They want a human sentence within a couple of rings, and if they get a
recording they hang up and dial the next result.

Any answering service worth paying for has to be good at exactly the moment you are least able to
answer — which is why the sales page matters much less than the escalation rules.

## The three kinds you can actually buy

### 1. A traditional live answering service (a call centre)

A room of operators somewhere answers as your business, follows a script you supply, takes a message,
and emails or texts it to you.

**Good at:** sounding human, handling volume, following an escalation rule if you write one.
**Bad at:** knowing anything about plumbing. The operator has never been under a sink. They read your
script, and if the caller says something the script didn't anticipate, the answer is usually "someone
will get back to you."

Watch the billing. Per-minute pricing means you pay for hold time, for the wrong numbers, for the
telemarketers, and for the four-minute call that turned out to be a supplier. Ask how they bill
non-customer calls before you sign anything.

### 2. A virtual receptionist

The same idea, further upmarket: a smaller team, sometimes a named person, working from a longer
script with more authority. Some will book jobs into a calendar and take payment details.

**Good at:** the polished front-desk impression, complex handling.
**Bad at:** the price. This is the most expensive option per call by a wide margin, and it is still a
human with working hours and an inbox. After-hours usually means a different, cheaper overflow team
answering your line at 11pm — which is precisely the call you were buying coverage for. Ask directly
who answers at 2am.

### 3. An AI receptionist

A voice AI answers your line, holds an actual conversation, answers from information you gave it, takes
the job details, and passes the lead to you.

**Good at:** picking up on the first ring, at 3am, on Christmas Day, for a flat monthly cost that
doesn't punish a busy month. It never has an off night and never quits.
**Bad at:** judgement, if it's built badly. An AI that always produces an answer is worse than no AI —
a caller with a complaint or an unusual situation needs a person, and a confident wrong answer costs
you more than a message would have.

:::callout
The single best question to ask any AI answering service:
"What does it do when it doesn't know the answer?" If the honest answer is anything other than "it
stops and hands off to a human," keep looking. Improvising is the failure mode that turns a
recoverable call into a lost customer and a one-star review.
:::

## What it costs, honestly

Pricing in this category is deliberately hard to compare, because the three models hide cost in
different places.

| Model | Typical shape | You pay more when | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Per minute** | Base fee + roughly $1.00–$2.00/min | Calls run long, or you get lots of them | Hold time, wrong numbers and spam all billed as minutes; rounding up to the minute |
| **Per call** | Roughly $2–$5 per answered call | Volume climbs | What counts as a "call" — some bill hang-ups and robocalls |
| **Flat monthly** | Roughly $20–$200/mo | Rarely — that's the point | Whether there's a minutes cap, and what overage costs |
| **In-house (hiring)** | Wages + payroll tax + cover | Always, and it doesn't scale down | One person cannot cover 24/7; holidays and sick days are your problem |

Two traps in particular.

**"Unlimited" with an asterisk.** If a plan says unlimited anything, find the fair-use clause. There is
always one, and you will find it in the month you actually need the capacity.

**Per-minute maths that only works when you're quiet.** A per-minute plan looks cheap on a slow month
and expensive on a good one — which means your answering bill rises exactly when you're busiest and
have least time to audit it. Model a busy month, not an average one.

The comparison that matters isn't between two answering services. It's between
[what an answering service costs](/blog/answering-service-cost) and what one missed emergency
call costs. If your average emergency call-out is worth
$400 and a service costs $250 a month, it needs to save you slightly more than one call a month to be
free. Most plumbers know their own number for this instinctively.

## What to ask before you sign

Six questions, in order of how much money they will save you:

1. **"Who answers at 11pm on a Sunday, and are they the same team as at 10am Tuesday?"** For a lot of
   providers the honest answer is no. That's the call you're buying.
2. **"What exactly happens on an emergency? Walk me through it."** You want a specific escalation path
   — the questions asked, the threshold, and how your on-call tech is reached. If it's vague, it will
   be vague at 11pm too.
3. **"How do you bill a wrong number, a robocall, or a hang-up?"** On per-minute and per-call plans
   this is real money.
4. **"Can the person answering tell an emergency from a nuisance?"** Water actively running is a truck
   out now. A slow drip under a vanity is Thursday. Somebody has to know the difference, and it isn't
   the homeowner.
5. **"What happens to the details after the call?"** A message you have to re-key is a message you
   will lose. Ask what you actually receive and where it lands.
6. **"What's the cancellation term?"** Annual lock-ins are common in this category.

## The four questions your answering service should ask every caller

Whatever you buy, the script matters more than the brand. For a plumbing business, four answers decide
everything:

:::steps
Is water running right now? :: The single question that separates a same-night truck roll from a booking. If yes, everything else is secondary.
Do you know where the shut-off is? :: Talking a panicking homeowner to the stop tap protects the property and buys you the drive time.
What's the address? :: Not just for dispatch — travel distance decides whether this job is worth taking tonight at all.
Is this a rental? :: If a landlord or property manager has to approve the spend, the job is not booked yet no matter how urgent it sounds.
:::

An answering service that collects a name and a number has handed you a message. One that collects
those four answers has handed you a qualified job you can price before you get in the van.

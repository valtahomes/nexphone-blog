It is 9:40 on a Sunday night and a landscaper with three crew members is going through his phone bill at the kitchen table. Each seat is billed. An add-on for the AI receptionist sits on a separate line. A texting booster sits on another. He uses the phone to book mowing contracts, and he cannot name one feature on the invoice that he opened in the last month.

If that sounds like your bill, this guide is for you. It covers who to move to, what the move costs in features, and who should stay put.

## The short answer

**The best RingCentral alternative depends on what you use it for.** If you want a cheaper phone system with a shared team inbox, look at Quo or Nextiva. If you want live humans answering your calls, look at Smith.ai or Ruby, and expect to pay several times more. If you mostly need the phone answered while your hands are full, a flat-fee AI receptionist costs the least.

**What you give up by leaving:** video meetings, team chat, and a long integration list, unless the replacement has them too. RingCentral's own page lists 330+ integrations and claims 99.999% reliability for its phone service. Few small competitors match that breadth.

**What you save:** usually the seat count and the add-on stack. All prices below were read from vendor pages on 13 October 2026. They move, so check the linked page before you buy.

## Why small businesses leave RingCentral

RingCentral is built as a full communications platform. Phone, video, team messaging, contact centre and events live under one login. That suits a 50-person office. A solo electrician pays for a lot of surface area he never touches.

Three things on RingCentral's own plans page explain most of the friction:

- **Add-ons price separately.** The AI Receptionist starts at $39, Business SMS Booster is $25, and Call Queues Booster is $35. Each sits on top of a RingEX plan.
- **Per-seat pricing.** RingEX is priced by number of users, and the page says you can save up to 33% by paying annually. The plain-text version of the page I fetched did not show the per-seat dollar figures, so open the page and read them yourself before comparing.
- **Texting has conditions.** RingCentral says SMS is not available during the 14-day free trial, that TCR registration is required, and that additional TCR fees may apply.

None of this makes RingCentral a bad product. It makes it a product sized for a larger company, with a bill to match. If you want its numbers laid out, [RingCentral pricing](/blog/ringcentral-pricing) goes through them.

## What you give up when you leave

Check this before you cancel anything.

- **Video meetings and team chat.** If your crew or clients use them daily, a phone-only tool means paying for another app.
- **Integration depth.** RingCentral lists more than 330 integrations, including Microsoft Teams calling, Salesforce, HubSpot and Zendesk. A smaller vendor may cover only your CRM and a few others.
- **Scale.** Multi-site routing, cost centre management and analytics tiers matter once you have branches. They rarely matter at three people.
- **Hardware.** RingCentral supports desk phones. Several app-first alternatives do not.

Write down which of these you used in the last 30 days. If the answer is none, the move saves you money with little risk.

## The alternatives, priced

Everything here comes from the vendors' own pages, read on 13 October 2026. Treat these as shapes, not quotes.

### Option 1: another business phone app

**Quo (formerly OpenPhone)**

- Starter $19 per user per month, Business $33, Scale $47 on monthly billing. On annual billing those fall to $15, $23 and $35.
- One local or toll-free number per user, with calling and texting to US and Canadian numbers.
- Its Sona AI agent comes with 1,000 free credits, which Quo's page equates to 10 calls. More calls need a paid credit tier starting at $25 a month for 40 calls.
- Texting needs registration with a $19.50 one-time fee and $1.50 to $3 a month.
- Phone menus, call transfers and AI call summaries start on the Business plan.

A three-person team on Business pays $99 a month on monthly billing, or $69 on annual. There is more in [Quo pricing](/blog/quo-pricing) and [Quo alternatives](/blog/quo-alternatives).

**Nextiva**

- The Core plan is listed at $15 per user per month, shown against a crossed-out $23. The page text does not say which billing term the $15 assumes.
- Engage is $25 per user per month (crossed-out $50) and Scale is $75.
- XBert, its AI receptionist, is an add-on. The page also advertises unlimited calling plus XBert AI plans from $99 a month.
- Core covers voice, SMS, video meetings and team chat.

Three seats on Core come to $45 a month at the listed price, before any AI. The breakdown is in [Nextiva pricing](/blog/nextiva-pricing) and [Nextiva alternatives](/blog/nextiva-alternative).

### Option 2: pay humans to answer

**Smith.ai, human-first plans**

- Starter: $300 a month for 30 calls. Overage is $11.50 per call.
- Basic: $810 for 90 calls. Pro: $2,100 for 300 calls.
- No setup fees, no overseas agents, and spam calls are not charged when they pass caller ID.
- Month to month, with a 30-day money-back guarantee.

Sixty calls on Starter would cost $300 plus 30 × $11.50, which is $645. See [Smith.ai pricing](/blog/smith-ai-pricing) for the full list.

**Ruby**

- 50 minutes a month is $250, 100 minutes is $395, 200 minutes is $720, 500 minutes is $1,725.
- Receptionists are live and available 24/7, with scheduling and payment collection listed among the features.
- Ruby says there are no setup or activation fees.

### Option 3: an AI receptionist

**Smith.ai AI receptionist**

- Free tier: 25 calls a month, then $3.00 a call.
- Pro: $150 a month for 75 calls ($2.00 a call).
- Enterprise starts at $500 a month for 300 calls.
- Calls can escalate to its live agent network.

**Nextiva XBert and Quo's Sona** are the add-on versions, priced above.

## Who should stay on RingCentral

Stay if you have more than about 20 seats, run several locations, need desk phones, depend on the Microsoft Teams calling integration, or have a compliance requirement that its certifications cover. RingCentral's page lists HITRUST, GDPR, PCI and HIPAA. Leaving for savings of a few dollars a seat is rarely worth rebuilding that.

Stay as well if your team lives in its video and chat tools and the phone is one part of a bigger setup. Replacing three apps with three vendors rarely saves money.

## Questions to ask before you switch

1. **Can you port the number, and what happens to calls during the port?** Keep the old service until the port completes.
2. **What does texting cost on top of the plan?** Include the carrier registration fees and the days it takes.
3. **Is the AI included or an add-on?** Price the plan and the add-on together.
4. **What happens when the AI cannot answer?** The right answer is that it hands off to a person or voicemail.
5. **What is the cancellation term?** Month to month beats annual if you are unsure.
6. **Which of your current integrations disappear?** Check the specific CRM or calendar you use.

For anything involving texting, read [10DLC registration](/blog/10dlc-registration) first. The paperwork is the same everywhere, and it is the step most owners miss.

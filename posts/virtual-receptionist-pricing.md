## The short answer

**What it costs:** a live virtual receptionist, on menus published 25 August 2026, starts around **$250 a month** for a small minute bundle (Ruby, 50 minutes) and climbs to **$2,100 a month** for a 300-call live plan (Smith.ai Pro). Per-call live overage on Smith.ai sits at **$8.50–$11.50 a call**. AI receptionist pricing is a different shelf. Smith.ai's AI Pro plan is **$150 a month** for 75 calls at **$2.00 a call**. Flat phone-plus-AI plans sit lower still, billed by the month rather than by the conversation.

**What you're choosing:** a per-minute live service, a per-call live service, or a flat / per-call AI receptionist. Same search phrase. Three invoices.

**What to judge it on:** the cost of *your* busy month, including the calls that were never jobs, and what you actually get on the call — a message, a qualified lead, or a booked appointment.

## What "virtual receptionist pricing" covers

People use the phrase for three products that do not bill the same way.

A **live virtual receptionist** is a trained person answering as your business, usually 24/7, from a script you supply. Ruby and Smith.ai's human-first plans are this. You are paying for labour on the line.

A **traditional answering service** is the older cousin, a call centre taking messages, often priced per minute, built for volume more than intake. The industry-wide version of that bill is in [what an answering service costs](/blog/answering-service-cost).

An **AI receptionist** answers with a voice model, from information you gave it, and passes the job to you. Some vendors sell this next to their live plans. Some sell only this. [AI receptionist pricing](/blog/ai-receptionist-pricing) is its own menu. It keeps landing in this search because buyers comparing "virtual receptionist" quotes now get both.

The phrase is commercial. You are shopping. Treat every number below as a shape published on a dated page, not a bid for your account. Vendors change plans. Pull the live page before you sign.

## Per-minute pricing

You buy a bucket of receptionist minutes. Every answered second eats the bucket. Go over, and you buy more minutes or pay overage.

**Ruby**, published 25 August 2026 on ruby.com/plans-and-pricing:

- 50 minutes — **$250 / month**
- 100 minutes — **$395 / month**
- 200 minutes — **$720 / month**
- 500 minutes — **$1,725 / month**

Effective rate if you use the whole bucket: about **$5.00 a minute** on the 50-minute plan, **$3.95** on 100, **$3.60** on 200, **$3.45** on 500. Ruby's page says the plans include 24/7 live answering, and that activation, onboarding, setup and after-hours coverage are not extra line items. Optional work they list on every plan includes scheduling, bilingual answering, client intake and payment collection.

Chat is a separate ladder on the same date: **$143 / month** for 10 chats, **$335** for 30, **$520** for 50. Bundling chat with a receptionist plan is listed at 20% off those chat prices.

What per-minute punishes:

- Hold time
- The caller who wants to tell the whole story
- Wrong numbers and anyone the operator stays on the line with
- Rounding. A 61-second call that bills as two minutes is a different product from a 61-second call that bills as 61 seconds.

What per-minute forgives: a short, clean job call. A 40-second "are you open Saturday" is cheap on this model and expensive on a per-call plan.

A 200-minute Ruby month at $720 is roughly **80 calls** if they average 2.5 minutes. That is a useful yardstick. If your real average is 4 minutes, the same plan covers about 50 conversations and the next rung is $1,725.

Ask, before you sign, how they bill a robocall, a hang-up, and time spent on hold. Those three answers move the monthly number more than the headline rate.

## Per-call pricing

You buy a bundle of answered calls. Length of the conversation mostly drops out of the maths. Volume does not.

**Smith.ai live receptionists**, published 25 August 2026 on smith.ai/pricing/receptionists:

- Starter — **30 calls for $300 / month**, overage **$11.50** a call, one free transfer destination
- Basic — **90 calls for $810 / month**, overage **$10.50** a call, two free transfer destinations
- Pro — **300 calls for $2,100 / month**, overage **$8.50** a call, ten free transfer destinations
- Enterprise — custom

Included rate on those three published tiers: **$10.00**, **$9.00**, and **$7.00** a call. Overage is higher than the included rate on every tier, which is the usual shape — the plan looks tidy until month eight, when a Google ad works and you blow past 30.

Smith.ai's page, same date, says spam and unsolicited sales calls are not counted if the forwarded lines pass Caller ID. Month-to-month. 30-day money-back on the initial plan, up to $1,000, with overage excluded from the refund. A 12-month prepay is listed at 10% off.

Add-ons on that page are where a $300 plan stops being $300:

- Book an appointment — **$1.50** a call
- Accept a payment — **$1.00** a call
- SMS or Slack ping on a qualified transfer — **$0.50**, plus **$0.25** per extra ping
- Call recording and transcription — **$0.25** a call
- Dedicated Spanish line — **$1.00** a call
- Text and email follow-up — **$0.50** a call
- Extra transfer destinations — **$15 / month** each
- Inbound tracking numbers — **$5 / month** each

A Starter month of 30 calls with booking and a payment on half of them is $300 + (15 × $1.50) + (15 × $1.00) = **$337.50** before anyone goes over the bundle. Still cheap against a hire. Easy to miss on the sales page.

What per-call punishes: high volume, short calls, and any vendor that counts a hang-up as a call. What it forgives: a long intake. An eight-minute new-customer call costs the same as a 90-second one.

Smith.ai's own FAQ on that page says they bill per call so they are not rewarded for keeping people on the line. That is the honest case for this model. The honest case against it is overage at $11.50 when a slow month turns into a busy one.

- Free — **$0 / month**, 25 calls, then **$3.00** a call
- Pro — **$150 / month**, 75 calls at **$2.00** a call (~2–3 calls a day on their copy). Larger published bundles on that tier: 150 calls at **$1.80**, 300 at **$1.67**

## A month of real calls, priced three ways

Use one busy month, not an average.

Say you logged **80 inbound conversations** that were actual people, averaging **2.5 minutes**, plus junk the vendor may or may not bill. You do not need a calendar booked on the line. You need the call answered and the job on your phone.

**Ruby-shaped, per minute.** 80 × 2.5 = 200 minutes. That is the **$720 / month** plan on Ruby's 25 August 2026 menu, if you land exactly on 200 and junk is short. Average 3.5 minutes instead and you need the 500-minute plan at **$1,725**, or you are in overage they do not publish as a simple extra-minute rate on that page. Ask.

**Smith.ai-shaped, per call.** 80 calls sits under the 90-call Basic plan at **$810 / month**. Starter plus 50 overage calls is $300 + (50 × $11.50) = **$875**. Basic is cheaper than overflowing Starter. That pattern repeats at every tier — jump the plan before you live on overage.

**Smith.ai AI-shaped.** 80 calls on the $150 Pro plan is 75 included + 5 extras at $2.50 = **$162.50**, or you take the 150-call bundle they list at a lower per-call rate. Same vendor, same month, roughly a fifth of the live bill.

Now flip the job. You want every qualified caller booked into a calendar and a card taken on half the jobs. Ruby lists scheduling and payment collection as available on the minute plans above. Smith.ai live lists them as **$1.50** and **$1.00** per call. On 40 booked, 40 paid Smith.ai live calls you add $60 + $40 = **$100** to the month. The AI column only stays cheap if you do that booking yourself after the text lands.

The comparison that matters is still the missed job, not the two quotes against each other. A plumber's after-hours ticket is a different economic event. [Angi's ranges and the 11pm test](/blog/answering-service-for-plumbers) walk through that trade. A cleaner, a landscaper, a one-truck HVAC shop: write down what one missed booked day is worth. Then look at $162, $720, and $810 against that number. Most owners already know which invoice a single lost Saturday wipes out.

## What the quote leaves off

Four places a tidy monthly number comes apart.

**Overage.** Smith.ai live Starter overage is $11.50 against an included $10.00. Going 10 calls over a 30-call plan adds $115. The Basic plan at $810 would have covered 90. Do the crossover before you pick the small plan to "see how it goes."

**Junk.** Per-minute vendors bill time. A two-minute robocall is two minutes. Per-call vendors vary. Smith.ai live says spam does not count when Caller ID is clean. Smith.ai AI says known spam is blocked and up to 10% of the rest can be stripped. Anyone who cannot answer this in one sentence will explain it on the invoice instead.

**Add-ons you will actually use.** Recording, a Spanish line, a second transfer destination, booking, a payment. Smith.ai publishes those rates. Ruby folds several of them into the plan and says so. A quote that is $200 cheaper with booking sitting on the extras list is not cheaper.

**The night team.** Ruby and Smith.ai both publish 24/7 at the same rate as daytime on the pages above. Plenty of older answering services still route nights to a different, cheaper overflow shop. [After-hours answering](/blog/hvac-answering-service) is the whole product for a lot of trades. Ask who is on the line at 11pm on a Sunday, by name of team, and whether that team has your script.

:::steps
Pull a busy month of call logs :: Count real conversations, average length, and junk. Use the high month, not the mean.
Price the same month three ways :: Per-minute against your total minutes. Per-call against your total conversations plus published overage. AI against the published bundle you would actually sit in.
Add the extras you will tick :: Booking, payments, recording, a second destination, a Spanish line. If you will not use them, leave them off. If you will, they belong in the number you compare.
Ask the night-and-junk questions in writing :: Who answers at 11pm, and what a robocall costs. Keep the email.
:::

## Hiring a person instead

The [Bureau of Labor Statistics *Occupational Outlook Handbook* for receptionists](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/receptionists.htm) (May 2024 wage data) puts the median at **$17.90 an hour**. Forty hours a week is about **$37,200 a year** before payroll tax, time off, and the afternoon they are out sick. That person covers one shift. Lunch is uncovered. Saturday is uncovered. The 7pm call from someone who wants a quote is voicemail.

A live virtual receptionist at $300–$810 a month is the night, the lunch break, and the second line. A hire is the right buy when you also need a body in the room: packages, walk-ins, a waiting area. It is the wrong buy when the job is "the phone rings while both hands are in a customer's house."

Smith.ai's live FAQ, 25 August 2026, puts their Pro plan about $33,000 under a yearly in-house receptionist salary. The BLS number above is the one to do that sum with for your own zip code. Wage medians move. Check the handbook date.

## Six questions that change the price

Ask these in this order. Each one has cost a shop real money.

1. **What is a billable unit?** A minute, a call, a "conversation," a transferred call. Get the definition in writing, including hang-ups.
2. **What does a robocall cost me?** Time, a full call, or nothing. Ask for last month's junk rate on an account the size of yours.
3. **Who answers at 11pm Sunday, and is it the same team as Tuesday at 10am?** Same rate is not the same team.
4. **Walk me through overage.** The per-unit price, when it kicks in, and whether you can jump plans mid-month.
5. **Which extras are in the number you just quoted?** Booking, payments, recording, bilingual, a second destination. Have them price the quote again with the extras you will actually switch on.
6. **What is the cancellation term?** Smith.ai live is month-to-month through the current cycle. Smith.ai AI asks for 30 days' notice. Ruby's pricing page on 25 August 2026 does not state a term in the public copy. Ask.

Do not skip question 3. A virtual receptionist that is excellent at 10am and outsourced at 11pm is two products on one invoice.

It is 2:15 on a Thursday afternoon. A landscaper is behind a mower on the third lawn of the day, ear protection on, phone buzzing in a pocket he can't reach. By the time he shuts the engine off, the call has gone to voicemail and the homeowner has dialed the next name on Google.

That night he sees an ad for Rosie, an AI answering service, and types "rosie ai receptionist" into the search bar. He wants three answers. What does it cost, what does it do, and is there anything better for a one-truck business?

:::callout
**Which Rosie this covers.**
Two unrelated products share the name. This guide is about Rosie at heyrosie.com, the AI answering service. The other one, at rosie.click, is a different company and gets no further mention here.
:::

## The short answer

Rosie costs **$49 a month** for 250 minutes (Professional), **$149** for 1,000 minutes (Scale), or **$299** for 2,000 minutes (Growth). Those prices come from heyrosie.com/pricing as read on 7 October 2026, and they can change.

All three plans answer calls 24/7 with an AI voice that learns your business from your Google profile and website, then sends you summaries, transcripts and recordings. The plan you pick mostly decides what happens after the call. Professional texts the caller an appointment link. Scale books straight onto your calendar and transfers live calls to you. Growth adds backup transfer paths and hands-on onboarding.

For a solo trade business with modest call volume, the $49 plan is a cheap way to stop losing calls. If you want jobs booked without the caller doing anything, the real price starts at $149.

## What Rosie does on every plan

The pricing page lists these as included on all three plans:

- 24/7 answering, with multiple calls handled at the same time, so no busy signals
- Training from your Google business profile and website, plus a custom FAQ you write yourself
- Ten-plus agent voices, a custom agent name and greeting, and English and Spanish on every call
- Your choice of area code
- Call summaries, transcripts and recordings
- Email and text notifications for each call
- Native iOS and Android apps
- Automatic spam detection
- A website chat widget
- Zapier, which connects Rosie to other apps

Rosie also claims more than 2,000 small businesses on its books. That figure comes from Rosie's own marketing, and nobody has audited it.

The spam filter matters more than it sounds. If you run ads or have a listing that attracts sales calls, you don't want an AI burning paid minutes on a robocall. Rosie says it detects spam automatically. The page doesn't say whether spam counts against your minutes, so ask.

## Rosie pricing, plan by plan

**Professional, $49 a month.**
250 minutes. Message-taking for two scenarios, meaning two call types you script, such as "new job inquiry" and "existing customer." It sends appointment links by text, which means the caller books themselves after the call. This is the plan for an owner who wants every call answered and every lead captured, and who is happy to call back and close.

**Scale, $149 a month.**
1,000 minutes. Message-taking for five scenarios. It books appointments directly on your calendar. It does warm transfers, where Rosie briefs you before connecting the call, and live transfers, which connect immediately. It can also text the caller during the call. This is the first plan where the AI does the whole front-desk job for a business with a schedule.

**Growth, $299 a month.**
2,000 minutes. Unlimited scenarios. Waterfall transfers, where Rosie tries several numbers until someone answers. Custom agent training from your uploaded documents and policies. White-glove onboarding and priority support. This one is built for businesses with a dispatcher's worth of call volume.

**Add-ons.**
Website chat is free on all plans. A separate website texting add-on costs $50 a month and includes 25 conversations, then $1 for each extra one.

**Annual billing.**
The page advertises two months free when you pay yearly. I didn't see the yearly dollar figures in the text I read, so do that math at checkout.

## What the pricing page leaves open

Four things to settle before you hand over a card.

**What a minute means.**
Rosie bills in minutes. A two-minute call that ends with a booked job uses two. A four-minute call from a salesperson uses four, unless the spam filter catches it first. Check how Rosie rounds, and whether hold or ring time counts.

**What happens when you go over.**
The FAQ lists a question about exceeding your minutes. The answer wasn't in the text I read. Get it in writing, because on a busy month this is the number that moves your bill.

**Whether your number carries over.**
Rosie lets you pick an area code, which suggests a new number by default. If the number is already on your truck and your Google listing, ask how forwarding works with your carrier.

**Calendar booking sits on the $149 plan.**
If you picked Rosie for the booking, budget for Scale, not Professional.

## Who Rosie suits

Rosie fits an owner who wants a cheap, fast answer to the missed-call problem and is fine with the AI's job ending at "got the details, here's a summary." At $49 with a free trial, the risk is small. Run a week of real calls through it and read the transcripts.

It fits less well if you need a person on the line for sensitive calls, or if your business needs the AI to hand off to a human agent network when it gets stuck. Rosie's page describes transfers to your own team, not to a staffed call center.

## Questions to ask before you sign

Whichever service you pick, test it the way a customer would.

1. **Call it from a different phone at 10pm.** Ask something your FAQ doesn't cover. See whether it admits it doesn't know or invents an answer.
2. **Say something urgent.** "My basement is flooding" should reach you faster than "I'd like a quote next month."
3. **Check the text you receive.** Does the summary contain the address, the problem and a callback number, or do you have to open a transcript to find them?
4. **Ask about overage and rounding in writing.**
5. **Ask what happens to your number if you leave.**

The first test is the one that separates services. An AI that guesses at an answer costs you more than a plain voicemail would.

## The short answer

**Quo alternatives fall into three piles.** Another team phone (Google Voice, Grasshopper, Nextiva). A live receptionist (Ruby, Smith.ai's human plans). An AI receptionist that answers when nobody on the crew can.

**What Quo costs:** [quo.com/pricing](https://www.quo.com/pricing) on 23 September 2026 lists Starter at **$19 per user / month**, Business at **$33**, Scale at **$47**. Yearly billing is **$15 / $23 / $35**. Every plan includes **1,000 Sona credits**, and Quo counts **100 credits as one AI call** — so the included AI answering is **10 calls a month**. Extra Sona packs start at **$25** for 40 calls.

**Who should stay.** A small office that already likes the shared inbox, does not need the phone answered at 9pm, and is fine paying per head. Quo is a phone system that does that job.

**Who should leave.** Anyone whose real problem is missed jobs after hours, or a crew of three or four people watching the per-user line item grow faster than the work. Then you are shopping a receptionist, not a cheaper dial tone.

## What Quo actually is

Quo is a cloud phone for small teams. Each paid user gets a number. You share inboxes, ring several people, send texts from the business line, and put a menu in front of callers on the higher plans. The AI piece is **Sona**, described on the same pricing page as an agent that answers calls 24/7.

That mix is why the comparison gets messy. You can use Quo as:

- A second number on your cell, so customers never get your personal line
- A shared line for a few people
- A light after-hours answerer, until the credits run out

Quo is not a call centre or a person taking a card over the phone. Sona is billed in credits. Read the credit table before you treat after-hours coverage as included.

On Starter, AI summaries and transcripts apply to Sona-handled calls. Business ($33/user) adds summaries on all calls, phone menus, auto recording, and HubSpot / Salesforce. Scale ($47/user) adds AI call tags, dedicated onboarding, and inbound phone support. All of that is from the 23 September 2026 pricing page.

Calling and messaging to US and Canadian numbers is listed as unlimited, subject to a fair-use policy. Extra numbers are **$5/month**. Carrier registration for US texting is a separate pass-through: **$19.50** one-time and **$1.50–$3/month**.

## What a month of Quo really costs

The sticker is per user. Model heads, then model Sona.

**Three people on Business**

- 3 × $33 = **$99/month** for the phones
- Sona included = 10 AI calls
- A shop that wants ~100 after-hours pickups buys the **$49** pack (10,000 credits / 100 calls on that page)
- Running total: **$148/month**, before extra numbers and carrier fees

**Solo on Starter**

- **$19/month**
- 10 Sona calls included
- The **$25** pack adds 40 AI calls (4,000 credits)
- Running total: **$44/month** if you use the AI

Overage on Sona, if you blow past the pack, is listed at **$1.00 / $0.75 / $0.65 / $0.55 / $0.45** per extra call as you move up the five credit tiers.

Two things hide in that maths.

**Users are the meter.** Add a helper for the summer and the phone bill steps up by $19, $33 or $47, every month, even if that person barely takes calls. Receptionist products meter minutes or calls instead. Phone products meter seats. Pick the meter that matches how you work.

**Ten AI calls is not after-hours coverage.** A plumber or HVAC tech who gets a handful of evening calls a week will burn the included Sona bucket in the first fortnight. The pack is the real AI price. Treat the 1,000 credits as a trial, not a plan.

If you want the wider market (per-minute vs per-call vs flat), that breakdown lives in [what an answering service costs](/blog/answering-service-cost). Quo sits on the phone-system side of that line.

## Why people look for Quo alternatives

Four patterns, from the same kind of owner.

**The bill grew with headcount.** Two techs became four. The shared inbox is still useful. The per-user total is not.

**After-hours was the actual problem.** They bought a business number so they could stop handing out a cell. The cell still rings, or the business line hits voicemail at 7pm, because Sona was never funded past the included 10 calls.

**They wanted a receptionist and bought a phone.** A menu and a shared inbox do not qualify a burst pipe. They take a name. The owner still has to call back and ask the questions that decide whether a truck rolls.

**They need a person for some calls.** Payments, a complaint, a caller who will not talk to a bot. Quo does not staff a live desk. Smith.ai and Ruby do.

None of those mean Quo is a bad product. They mean the job changed.

## How the bills actually differ

Ignore the homepages for a minute. The meter is the product.

:::steps
Per user (Quo, Google Voice, Grasshopper) :: You pay for people. Quiet techs still cost a seat. After-hours AI, if it exists, is usually a second meter on top.
Per minute (Ruby) :: You pay for talk time, including the calls you wish had not happened. Fine on a quiet line. Painful on a busy one.
Per call (Smith.ai) :: You pay for answered customer calls. Spam policy matters a lot. Overage is the number to model.
Credits (Quo Sona) :: A phone plan with a thin AI bucket. Cheap until you treat it as 24/7 coverage, then you are buying packs.
Flat AI minutes :: A monthly cap on how long the voice AI can talk, often with a cheap base phone plan underneath. Busy months do not change the receptionist line the same way per-call does.
:::

There is no universally cheaper shape. A two-person office that almost never needs after-hours pickup will often spend less on a per-user phone. A one-van trade that lives on evening calls will often spend less on a receptionist, live or AI, and keep the phone simple.

## What to check before you switch

Do this in order. Skipping the first question wastes a week.

1. **Are you replacing a phone, or buying a pickup?** If the shared inbox is working and the pain is 7pm, keep the phone and add answering, or move to a product that is answering-first. If the pain is seats and you do not care about 7pm, another phone is enough.
2. **Who answers at 9pm on a Sunday, and what does that cost on a busy month?** For Quo the honest answer is Sona, metered. For Ruby it is a live operator, metered in minutes. For Smith.ai it is a person or an AI, metered per call. Get the overage in writing.
3. **What happens to the number?** Port it to the new provider *before* you cancel the old one. Forwarding carries calls and drops texts. If you are starting from scratch rather than moving a line, [how to get a business phone number](/blog/how-to-get-a-business-phone-number) covers local vs toll-free without the vendor pitch.
4. **When do texts actually start?** US carriers require 10DLC registration for outbound business SMS. Quo lists **$19.50** plus **$1.50–$3/month**. Other providers pass through similar fees. Calls and inbound voicemail should work from day one; outbound texting waits on the carriers. The process is [10DLC registration](/blog/10dlc-registration).
5. **What do you receive after the call?** A push into a shared inbox is a conversation. A text with the address, the problem, and a transcript is a job. If you also want a missed call to get a text back automatically, that is a separate feature — see [missed-call text-back](/blog/missed-call-text-back).
6. **Cancellation.** Quo, Smith.ai and Grasshopper all sell month-to-month on the pages above. Still ask. Annual prepay discounts (Quo’s yearly prices, Smith.ai’s 10% for 12 months on the human plans) are cheaper until you want out in week six.

Test one after-hours call yourself. Everything else is a brochure.

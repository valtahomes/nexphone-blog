It is 4:40 on a Thursday. You just finished a water-heater swap, you are still in the driveway, and the next stop texts: still coming? You reply from the business number you set up this morning. The message does not show as delivered. You try once more. Same result. You call. They do not pick up.

That is 10DLC. A carrier rule, not a setting you forgot.

:::callout
**Calls and inbound texts do not wait on 10DLC.**
Outbound business SMS does. Until your provider has filed your brand and campaign, quotes, “on my way” texts, and missed-call text-back sit in limbo. The rest of the phone line works.
:::

## The short answer

**What it is.** 10DLC means 10-digit long code, an ordinary US local number, the kind on a van and a Google listing. Registration is how AT&T, T-Mobile, Verizon and the rest decide a business is allowed to send SMS from that number. The records live at [The Campaign Registry](https://www.campaignregistry.com/) (TCR). Your phone provider files them.

**What it costs.** Fees come from the carriers and TCR. Your provider collects them as a pass-through. You do not open a TCR account and you do not get a bill from campaignregistry.com. One published pass-through, as of 22 September 2026, is about **$19 one-time plus about $2 a month**. Other providers set their own line item. Ask for it before you sign.

**How long.** It is not instant. TCR checks the brand and the campaign, then carriers use that file to let messages through. [The Campaign Registry](https://www.campaignregistry.com/) (fetched 22 September 2026) says messages go once your provider has a Campaign ID with their connectivity partner. Ask your provider for their current turnaround. Do not plan on sending a blast the afternoon you buy the number.

**Who files it.** You do not. TCR says brands cannot register directly. A Campaign Service Provider, the company that gave you the number, does the filing.

## What 10DLC registration is

A 10-digit long code is a normal phone number. Ten digits, local area code, the number customers already know how to dial. Businesses like it because it looks like a real office, not a 5-digit short code and not a 1-800 line.

Carriers treat texts from that number in two different ways.

**Person-to-person (P2P).** Two people texting from consumer phones. Low volume, back and forth, no script.

**Application-to-person (A2P).** A business sending SMS on purpose: appointment reminders, “we’re 20 minutes out,” a quote, a missed-call reply. Even if a human typed the message, carriers class it as A2P once it is coming from a business line at business volume.

10DLC is the A2P path for local numbers. Sanctioned 10DLC, in TCR’s words, is an A2P channel where brands and Campaign Service Providers are verified before they are allowed to send. Carriers want to know **who** is sending and **what** the messages are about, up front.

The product is a registry entry, not a new phone, a new area code, or a spam filter you install.

If you wanted a different kind of number altogether, [a toll-free business number](/blog/toll-free-number-for-business) is a separate carrier process. This page is the local-number SMS path.

## Why carriers made you do this

Unregistered 10-digit numbers were an open door. A scam text looks a lot like a plumber saying the part is in. Carriers got the blame, so they built a gate.

The gate has three claims, from TCR’s own description of the ecosystem (fetched 22 September 2026):

- The brand is known.
- The company sending on that brand’s behalf is known.
- The content category is known before the first message leaves.

For a one-truck shop that is not a philosophy problem. You are not a botnet. You still have to go through the same door, because the carriers do not inspect your van. They inspect the registry.

Skip it and outbound SMS fails in boring ways. Messages do not deliver. They deliver hours later. They land in spam. Customers think you ignored them. You think the phone is broken. The phone is fine. The campaign is missing.

## Brand, campaign, and the CSP

Three names show up on every 10DLC form. They are not three products. They are three rows in the same filing.

**Brand.** The legal business. The name on the insurance, the LLC paperwork, the Google listing if you kept them consistent. TCR holds the brand so a carrier can see who claims the traffic.

**Campaign.** What you will send. Appointment reminders are a campaign. Two-way customer support is a campaign. Mixing “we’re on the way” with a promotional blast you never described is how filings get messy. Describe the texts you actually send.

**Campaign Service Provider (CSP).** The phone or messaging company in the middle. TCR’s site is blunt: direct registration with TCR is not available for brands. You pick a CSP from their list, or you already have one because you bought a number from them. The CSP registers the brand and the campaign. After TCR issues a Campaign ID, the CSP gives that ID to their connectivity partner, and outbound SMS can start.

You will not log into campaignregistry.com and complete this. If a sales page tells you to, they have not read TCR’s own brand instructions.

:::steps
You buy or port a 10-digit business number :: The CSP issues the line. Calls and inbound texts can work immediately.
The CSP collects your business details :: Legal name, how you use SMS, the basics they need to describe the brand and campaign.
They file brand and campaign with TCR :: You do not file. They do.
Carriers use that file :: Outbound SMS is allowed once the Campaign ID is with the connectivity partner.
You send from the same number you already publish :: Van, invoices, Google listing. The number does not change. The permission to text does.
:::

## What works before registration clears

**Works without 10DLC**

- Inbound calls
- Outbound calls
- Voicemail
- Receiving a text a customer sends you

**Waits on 10DLC**

- You texting a customer first
- Quotes, reminders, “on my way,” job updates
- [Missed-call text-back](/blog/missed-call-text-back) — that reply is an outbound SMS, so it uses the same campaign

If your hands are full and the phone is ringing, you can still take the job. You can still call the customer back. You cannot yet fire the text that says you are 15 minutes out. Plan the first week around calls. Turn the texts on when the CSP says the campaign is live.

Forwarding an existing voice line to a new provider carries calls. It does not carry SMS. If the number on the van has to send texts, that number has to sit on the CSP who filed the campaign, which usually means a port, not a forward. The mechanics of getting the line sit in [how to get a business phone number](/blog/how-to-get-a-business-phone-number).

## What 10DLC registration costs

There is no public menu on campaignregistry.com for a shop owner to check out with a card. TCR bills CSPs. CSPs bill you. That is why two providers can quote two different numbers for the same underlying process.

What you can pin down without guessing:

- The fee is a **pass-through**, not a software feature. You are not buying extra AI minutes. You are paying the registry and the carriers to let A2P traffic off a 10-digit number.
- It is **separate from the phone plan**. Minutes, seats, and AI answering are one bill. 10DLC is another line.
- It is **not optional** if you want outbound SMS from that local number. There is no legitimate shortcut that skips TCR for US carrier delivery.
- Amounts **move**. An undated screenshot from a forum thread is already wrong.

Do not confuse this with per-message carrier surcharges some CSPs also pass through on each SMS. Those are a different line, and this page will not invent them. If your contract has a per-text carrier fee, it should say so in writing.

## How the registration happens

From your side the work is short. From the carrier side it is a review. You only see the first part.

You sign up for a business number with a CSP. You give them the legal business name and a plain description of the texts you send — job updates, missed-call replies, appointment times. They submit the brand and the campaign. You wait. They tell you when outbound SMS is allowed.

TCR’s own “how it works” note (fetched 22 September 2026): campaigns are registered through the CSP portal or APIs, and messages are sent after the Campaign Registry user gives their connectivity partner the Campaign ID. That sentence is the timeline you have. A file moving from TCR to the companies that deliver SMS.

On NexPhone the wait is a few business days, and they are explicit that **receiving texts and taking calls works right away**. Outbound SMS is the piece that needs the filing. That matches the carrier rule, not a house rule.

While you wait, use the voice line. Answer, call back, put the job on the calendar. The texting inbox is not approved yet.

## What to have ready

TCR does not publish a shop-owner checklist on the page we fetched. This is what a CSP has to know to describe you.

**Legal business name.** Match the name you use at the bank and on invoices as closely as you can. A cute van name with a different LLC behind it is how filings stall.

**What the texts will say, in one sentence.** “We confirm appointments and send on-the-way updates to customers who called us.” That is a campaign. “Whatever we feel like sending” is not.

**The number that will send.** Local 10-digit, on that CSP. A personal cell you also use for the kids’ school thread is a poor brand story and a poor operational one.

That is enough to start. Your CSP will ask for anything else their current form requires. Give them real answers. A campaign that says “customer care” and then sends discount blasts is the filing you will have to redo.

## What stalls a filing

Two failures show up before any mysterious carrier rejection.

**You try to register as a brand on campaignregistry.com.** TCR says you cannot. The site tells you to pick a CSP. Close the tab and go back to the company that issued the number.

**You send outbound SMS before the Campaign ID is in place.** TCR’s sequence is file, then ID, then traffic. Sending first is how messages vanish and you spend an afternoon testing the phone. The phone is not the fault.

Everything else is on the CSP and the carriers. If a provider cannot tell you whether they are a CSP, whether they file 10DLC, and what they charge to pass the fees through, you do not have a texting plan. You have a voice plan with hope attached.
